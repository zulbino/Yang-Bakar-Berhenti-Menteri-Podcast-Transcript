# Interview (English)

**Speaker 1:** We are on episode 26 with Rafizi Ramli. Before we start, please follow our social media accounts on Facebook, Twitter, IG, and YouTube. We’ve reached 1 million followers on Facebook—a significant milestone. I won’t prolong this, as we have many issues to cover this week. Let’s jump straight into it. Episode 26, first segment. Bismillah.

**Speaker 1:** So, we’ve heard about the "wave a hand and get RM100" incident. Do you know who this person is? Is this the one Papa Gomo is unhappy with? People call him "Sultan Pemurah" [Generous Sultan]. This is the first time I’ve come across him. He seems to be associated with what Papa Gomo calls "Tun M’s CyberTrooper." It’s not Chegu Bard, but someone people are quite angry at. The media is highlighting how he just hands out RM100 notes for waving. Do you know him?

**Speaker 2:** Not really. I’ve seen some things on social media, but I don’t follow it closely. It looks like he’s handing out "ang pow" [red packets].

**Speaker 1:** It’s risky, isn’t it? This happened in Klebang, Melaka. There is a risk when you distribute money like that because people will try to take it two or three times. That’s why you hear people in the background saying, "Only wave once, you get RM100." What’s the occasion, though? Is he naturally that generous? Some say he’s called "Sultan Pemurah" because he used to give out gifts during TikTok lives. Do you know how much a "Lion" gift costs?

**Speaker 2:** It’s over 2,000.

**Speaker 1:** Right, that’s how it works on TikTok. Well, maybe he’ll give us a Lion tonight, Ibi? Oh wait, we can't accept gifts because we are politicians.

**Speaker 2:** Even so, I’m generally uncomfortable with this approach. In our culture, especially in Islam, it is taught that when your right hand gives, your left hand should not know. Beyond that, while we shouldn’t judge someone’s intentions, this "wave and get RM100" trend promotes a feudal mindset in society. It reinforces the idea of a wealthy person at the top—who people might not even know or understand—handing out money to the masses. I feel uneasy when this becomes a culture, even during festivals like Raya. If one truly wants to help, it’s better to find people who are genuinely in need and help them quietly. I suspect this is just for "over-the-top" content.

**Speaker 1:** But he has political connotations. I believe he is a PKR member.

**Speaker 2:** That might have been after my time; I don’t know him. If we compare this to celebrities like Aishah Khalid, who also frequently distributes money—is that also considered "over-the-top"? I’d say that’s a matter of perspective. The public might appreciate it as a form of "sustenance," but I stick to the principle that charity should be discreet. When it becomes a culture where wealthy people feel they must perform these acts to be seen, we end up idolizing money. It might be effective for content, but it’s still fundamentally about the "content era."

**Speaker 1:** If waving one hand gets you RM100, what happens if you wave two hands? RM200?

**Speaker 1:** Next, we have the "Abang Grab Hero." This is a new category, Ibi—an antidote to last week's issues. We rarely see this; he’s clearing a clogged drain, which has nothing to do with him personally. He doesn’t even know where his motorcycle is, but it’s great that he’s helping. Often, these clogs are just caused by a little bit of surface trash. If everyone were this proactive, that would be great. This kind of "over-the-top" effort is perfectly fine.

**Speaker 1:** Now, Ibi, don’t be surprised. Cap Los is the most persistent. He used to praise you, right?

**Speaker 2:** That was back in 2019. Someone tagged me in this recently. What is wrong with his soul? He claims he mentions my name in his "recitations" every hour. I worry he’ll be saying my name even on his deathbed. It’s hard to get a picture with the family, but this guy is... something else. These characters come and go. I think he’s just doing it for the content.
