# Interview (English)

**Moderator:** Let us open the floor for one or two questions. Is there anyone who would like to ask?

**Hakim:** Assalamualaikum warahmatullahi wabarakatuh, YB Rafizi Ramli. I am Haziq Asfar (Hakim) from Tangga Batu, Melaka. I have traveled a long way just for you, YB Rafizi. My question: You mentioned that even if you lose, you will still hit the ground running to campaign actively. The question is, how do you plan to campaign if most division chiefs and the State Leadership Councils (MPN) reject you and issue statements supporting Saudari Nurul Izzah? Thank you.

**Rafizi Ramli:** As I mentioned earlier, that is their right. Young people sometimes get offended quickly; older people like me do not get offended at all. Those statements of support do not actually represent the grassroots. A Division Chief does not necessarily win 100% in their division, and many of those who win are like "a head without a body."

I understand this situation. If you recall in 2015 and 2016, I served as Vice President and Secretary-General of the party simultaneously—the only individual in this party to have held both positions at the same time. At that time, most of the other Vice Presidents were in Datuk Seri Azmin Ali's camp. In the Political Bureau, it was indeed tough for us to maneuver ("bersilat") because there were only me, Nik Nazmi, and Datin Paduka Tan Yew Keng defending our comrades. Most Division Chiefs genuinely did not like me, but that never stopped me. I just pushed forward regardless.

What matters most is bringing the voice of change to the people. Back then, that was why I brought a giant lorry to give speeches. For instance, in Pasir Gudang before YB Hassan Karim won. If the Division Chief at that time did not support me and refused to organize programs, I was not worried. I brought a lorry, parked it by the roadside, and the public would still turn up. When politicians see public support showing up, they will join in unabashedly. That is the way of politicians.

The higher our position in politics, the less we can afford to take offense. Life is tough. What matters most is the people. Ultimately, these branches still need speakers to win. Surely they wouldn't ask YB Ramanan to go and speak in their areas? So when they need me, I will still go and pretend to forget whatever they once said.

Just look at the example of Hassan Karim. He was not an original PKR member and joined the party after 2008 from PRM. After he lost in Ledang in 2013, I brought him to Pasir Gudang. We did crowdfunding, paid the deposit, and funded his campaign against Datuk Seri Khaled Nordin in 2018.

My advice to young people in politics: politics and life are indeed tough. In politics, we only have two judges. The first is the people, because real power and influence lie in the hands of the public out there, not merely a few Division Chiefs. The second judge is Allah SWT. If we hold fast to that principle and do not chase titles or positions, but instead seek the pleasure of Allah, Insya-Allah we will remain unshaken. If one road is closed, a new path will open.

**Moderator:** Alright, let us take one more question from the front. Please introduce yourself and ask your question.

**Rashdi bin Haji Pandak Ahmad:** Assalamualaikum warahmatullahi wabarakatuh. My name is Rashdi bin Haji Pandak Ahmad. I have been involved in the formation of Parti Keadilan since 1998. At that time, no outsiders were involved in elections—in fact, there were no elections; everything was voluntary. However, today, there are individuals who are not original PKR members, but rather from UMNO, who have won in the recent division elections. What are your views and actions regarding this matter, YB? I hope this issue is not just swallowed quietly, but voiced to defend the struggle of the veterans. Thank you.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Thank you, brother. I have long raised this issue within the party. My concern is that once we are in power, many rush to join. Because elections depend on the number of members brought in, individuals exist whom I refer to as "e-BNB members"—they merely check in briefly during elections and then disappear.

Whichever ones we could filter out early, we certainly objected to. For example, in Jempol, a re-election had to be held because the candidate who won as Division Chief was simultaneously a Pejuang Melaka committee member. How could such a person pass through party vetting?

On this matter, it is the responsibility of the Central Election Committee (JPP). I cannot intervene directly because as a contesting Deputy President, there is a conflict of interest. The JPP should be an independent body that vets all candidates. When there are objections, they must address them one by one, rather than ignoring them or only opening the objection process after the election is over. That move only angers the members.

As for the membership issue, it falls under the Secretary-General, Datuk Seri Fuziah Salleh. Objections regarding the infiltration of outside members must be met with firm action by the Secretary-General and the JPP.

My message to whoever holds the position of Chairman or Deputy Chairman of the JPP in the future: remember that you are the primary gatekeepers of the party to prevent such things from happening. Do not look down on complaints from grassroots members. Most of those who complain are old fighters who love the party deeply and want to protect it. As central leadership, I myself do not know every member at the lower levels. I need to trust old friends who understand the ground situation better.

Properly, when a complaint is received, check and investigate it in detail, call the relevant parties, request evidence, and resolve them one by one. If there is a shortage of manpower to vet, recruit more volunteers from among the party's old members.

If this is left unchecked, the damage will be done ("nasi sudah menjadi bubur"). By PRU16, our party will be filled with "e-BNB members" and e-BNB Division Chiefs. In the end, old members will feel hurt and sidelined because their voices were ignored when they stepped forward to speak out.

I take a large part of the responsibility and shortcomings for what happened. If I do not win in this election, I consider it a proper decree from Allah SWT, given that for the past two and a half years, my time has been overwhelmingly focused on my duties as a minister.
