I usually avoid riding on waves that are not my expertise so that I don't end up like this. If not careful, politicians sometimes like to show their faces, especially if the matter is currently hot. **Speaker 1:** Because this issue is sensitive, I don't have a specific question. As a politician, how do you respond to the issue of citizenship and these imported football players?

**Rafizi Ramli:** I think most of us were *husnuzon* (thought positively) at first when this issue started to arise because we did not have detailed information. Of course, the public has different views. There are two different things. One is about the strategy of using players who are seen as not Malaysian—imported players—to represent Malaysia. That has *pros and cons*. Some feel that if we bring in imported players to Malaysia, it will raise the quality, enable our teams to function more commercially, be more *sustainable*, and be able to compete at the regional level. Another view is that if our local football arena is seen as being too dominated by players who are not Malaysian, then it will drown out and close the opportunities for Malaysian players. I am not an expert on this matter, so that is a question of football. That’s why when the question of FIFA and all this came up, I didn't comment either because I didn't know and it is not a field I truly check.

But the second matter is related to the issue of citizenship, and that is not just a question of the player. The question is how the citizenship process was conducted, and it started to involve the government. The Minister of Home Affairs, Dato' Seri Saifuddin, when the answer was given, I think his answer, if summarized, was that he has used his power and rights as provided under the law after the committee and other processes were satisfied that they could be granted citizenship. *Now that is a public issue.* I didn't comment on that early on either. But when FIFA issued their evidence and their argument that they had already traced the birth documents, and there was evidence that *I think their grandparents* were not born in Malaysia, and were even registered in the Netherlands and other places, it raises questions about how the process took place and was conducted at the ministry level. Secondly, it embarrasses Malaysia. So, whatever answer the government gives, that is the responsibility of the ministry.
