# Interview (English)

**Haziq Asfar:** We can start. All right, good. *Bismillahirrahmanirrahim.* Peace be upon you all and greetings to everyone. Good evening, especially to YB Rafizi Ramli. How are you, YB? How are your cats doing?

**Rafizi Ramli:** I don’t know, I haven't seen them in a long time, I feel.

**Haziq Asfar:** That’s alright, YB, if your cats are healthy, then that’s fine. So, perhaps we can proceed with our session tonight. We don’t have much time, but so much has happened in the last week or two; it feels like things are falling apart. So, maybe others are wondering what exactly is going on. If I may start directly, in the past few weeks, there has been a lot in our news. We started with the decisions at the Keadilan branches, then it was reported by the newspapers that suddenly YB was on leave. Some even said YB had resigned. Do these things correlate? What is actually happening? Everyone is asking, YB.

**Rafizi Ramli:** *Assalamualaikum warahmatullahi wabarakatuh.* Are you guys not afraid to be here? Look, I actually don't pay much attention to the elections even though they started in early April, because my focus is on government work. We are going through many challenges, so we need to focus on that. From the beginning, the image we wanted to project was that we wanted to lower the temperature of the election at the grassroots level because we are in the government. We shouldn't show that the competition is spilling over to the people; it's better to keep it within the party.

But when the results started coming in, I thought perhaps the branches that had disputes would go to the President, Datuk Seri Anwar. But because the President is the Prime Minister, he has a lot of other work. In the end, many of them reached me from all groups, actually, not just from specific groups. When I started examining it, meaning there were issues arising, I decided that if I wanted to balance between working until seven or eight at night every day and then meeting branches at night, it just wouldn't work. We needed full time for me to sit down and meet one-on-one.

That is why I made the decision and told Datuk Seri Anwar to give me time to take leave from the office. Actually, that is not an issue at all. It is normal for personal matters, and actually, if we are a reformist party, personal matters, family matters, and party matters are not government business. So, you can't hold meetings or meet party people at the ministry. That is why I decided to go on leave so I could sit down and calm the situation. Besides, it’s not like that has never been done. I recall during Tunku Abdul Rahman’s time, when he wanted to campaign for the General Election, he took three months off from the government because he didn't want to mix government business with party business.

Maybe I am a bit "naive," I thought it was quite straightforward. The problem is, it’s also the problem of our own people. Those who go and provide unofficial sources or sources who do not want to be named include PKR ministers, too. I don’t know which minister went and spoke to the media until the issue suddenly became huge. Even though the matter is just normal. If I went to talk to the media every time I took leave, we’d be doomed. I think for now, the people are starting to understand, it’s not really an issue. My advice to other ministers is, if there are personal or party matters that require more time, don't mix up your duties and working hours for the government with the tasks of managing the party.

**Haziq Asfar:** That may be so, but the opposition is hammering you to death, YB. They say you are not in Parliament and all that.

**Rafizi Ramli:** It’s okay, they miss me. They call out to me so longingly; if I actually show up, well, ha.

**Haziq Asfar:** Okay YB, never mind. You mentioned the election patterns earlier. Not everyone watching our podcast is a party member. So when we talk about a "body without a head," some might not understand our election system. What is the phenomenon actually happening at our branches that so much news is coming out?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Well, this is the risk when a party becomes a government party. If we were only an opposition party, what happens in the party wouldn't affect the government. So when an issue arises, it naturally attracts the public's interest. I am usually conflicted because previously I never commented to the media at all. Good luck to the media, no matter how many times they tried to call, I really didn't comment because the matter needs to be managed internally as it has extended effects on the party. But we have entered the campaigning period, so this matter needs to be explained to the public who are watching, besides the party members themselves.

In Keadilan, or even in any party, when we compete in the General Election, we compete on the party ticket. People choose because they already know the whole team that will steer the administration. In Keadilan, it’s the same, every time there is an election there will be teams because we want to ensure that when they take office, they can start running immediately because the team is already in place. If it’s individuals, it will turn into a beauty pageant. When you want to steer the party as an organizational unit at the branch level, later Person A won't be on good terms with Person B, Person B won't like Person C. So logically and by practice, elections in Keadilan follow teams.

Usually, there are only two possibilities for the result. One, one team wins everything, and it's settled and they manage the branch. Or the second result is that the victory is mixed; some win certain positions, the committee members (AJK) are mixed. So we learn to coexist, find consensus to manage the party. That’s common sense. But this time, for the first time in PKR's history, we have a "head without a body" phenomenon. It means the number one position, the Branch Chief from this group wins, but numbers two down to the bottom are won by other people.

The votes are consistent. If the group from number two downwards gets 800 votes up to the 20th AJK member, suddenly the head who manages all of this loses 400 votes; he only gets 400. And those 400 go to the other one. The "head" without the body suddenly gets 900 votes, but everyone else in his group only gets 300 votes. If it happens in one or two places, we can call it an "anomaly"—perhaps a unique oddity there. But if it happens in almost 70 to 80 branches, especially in the first and second weeks, this phenomenon of a head without a body raises questions. That head will have no mandate from his entire committee. How is he going to manage the party effectively if he says A, and the whole committee says we don't want to follow?

That is why when I started looking—and those who work with me know I don't accept things at face value, I check the "chai" (slates) and so on—eventually, I felt this needed to be looked at. Especially since we started seeing a national pattern after two to three weeks. I told the party leadership, we could choose to close our ears as if none of this is happening, but we cannot control people’s feelings and understanding. Keadilan is a party that puts effort into educating the people and its members. If an issue arises, we must manage it well; don't ask people below to just swallow it whole. The process must happen. If those on the outside are scratching their heads wondering why PKR is suddenly noisy, it’s because there are things that I, as Deputy President, cannot deny. We have to manage it well and cannot avoid people saying things, especially against the backdrop of the national pattern that exists.

**Haziq Asfar:** Yes, so to summarize, our elections are indeed based on groups. Usually, if one group wins, everyone wins. But this time the head is one thing, the bottom is another. You mentioned the national pattern earlier—what trends have you observed across the country?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Well, by now we all know. There are those who have been "decapitated"; Nik is there, Akmal is there. Akmal is brave, he is sitting in front. I asked, even cabinet colleagues themselves looked and said it was strange. It has never happened before that a Deputy Minister, a Member of Parliament—if I take Akmal as an example—he has been the PKR Johor Bahru Branch Chief since 2014. He organized JB from an UMNO stronghold until he could take over from Tan Sri Shahrir Samad who had never lost in his life, with a 50,000 majority. Until he became a Deputy Minister, and I think as a Deputy Minister he wasn't that "useless," people saw an impact. I have never praised Akmal publicly, but this time I will do it publicly, he isn't that useless—there are other PKR Deputy Ministers who are more useless than him.

But suddenly, somehow, there is a kid we have never known, who has no permanent job in terms of qualifications, has no record, but can defeat Akmal even while Akmal's team won other positions. People see that pattern. Nik's case is worse; he was defeated by a professional, but he is a professional joker. So how are we supposed to answer that? In the party, you can make excuses, but eventually, questions arise as to why this is happening. The pattern is that anyone whose political style appears similar in approach to mine, or whose thinking is in line with me, no matter how great they are, they all lose in strange circumstances.

That is what you call strange results. Even then, I stayed silent, I said let’s manage it. But lately, part of the cartel culture that we had discarded has returned to the party. Every day there will be news—journalists listening to this must know—there is news about PKR from sources who do not want to be named. Have you brothers and sisters ever seen me make a press statement attacking people from sources who do not want to be named? If I want to say something, I say it directly. Those who release this news do it to build a national pattern; the pinnacle is the narrative that Rafizi has been rejected 100 percent by the branches and the party.

Because of that, supposedly I don't need to compete. If it were someone else, I think they would have said "never mind, I’ll go on leave, I’m not competing anymore." But I think this gang underestimates me. I understand everything that is happening. The pattern is organized to build a narrative that Rafizi is rejected by the grassroots. Then Ramanan comes in telling that story, someone else comes in telling that story. Eventually, the final narrative is that because I do not have support, there needs to be another candidate for Deputy President of the party. The people are observing these developments. We cannot blame the people if they feel that is indeed the national pattern and the narrative being built.

**Haziq Asfar:** It could be said that everyone associated with your team lost at the branch level. But some say this is a loss because you didn't take care of the grassroots. I am quoting news from today, YB Ramanan Ramakrishnan, a candidate for Vice President, said this defeat was caused by not taking care of the grassroots. He said you have bodyguards, you’ve become a minister, you don't even reply to WhatsApp. How about that, YB?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Well, I don't have WhatsApp; the whole world knows that. Even my family doesn't have WhatsApp. That is part of the narrative being built. I can't blame Ramanan, maybe he is new to this party. When we fought Najib over 1MDB in 2015, he was still the MIC Treasurer. He joined the party in 2020, meaning even after MIC lost in 2018 he was still with MIC. Maybe he is confusing the grassroots culture in Keadilan with his old party. Our culture is different.

What does "taking care of the grassroots" mean? If taking care of the grassroots is as Ramanan said—going down secretly, giving 5,000 to 10,000 to people so they support him—then I admit I don't take care of them like that. If taking care of the grassroots means sending Deepavali hampers, Raya hampers... Is Wong Chen here? Wong Chen once messaged me, he said, "Why are you making it hard for me?" I asked why? He said someone sent a truckload of Raya cookie boxes even though Raya was over a week ago, but because this is the "election Raya." He said it’s troublesome because he has to carry them up to the second-floor office.

If you want to say I didn't take care of the grassroots because of those things, I admit it. We are a reformist party. If I wanted to do that, I might as well have joined another party; it’s easier than having to get out of the lockup to go to demos. Ramanan has never felt being beaten at a demonstration; we have felt being sprayed with water and all that. Lately, when we have won and become the government, many characters from which party have entered, I don't even know. That culture is there, maybe he thinks I am indeed a bad Deputy President. But our culture in PKR, taking care of the grassroots means we go down to the ground to strengthen our friends so they are respected in their parliamentary areas. We can't hand out money because we don't have money either.

I noticed things that have never happened in the party; there are women leaders who want to compete until they buy headscarves worth hundreds of thousands. What is this? We never competed by giving away headscarves! We compete by strengthening the branch by bringing the people's agenda that garners support from ordinary people. We build confidence that this party is fit to govern because we are more honest, our economic plans are better, and we bring a transparent political culture. That is the responsibility of the central leadership. We go down to the branches to give speeches, meet the people, show our leadership so that when the election comes, our friends in the branch win. That is taking care of the grassroots, not spreading money. My record on that is better than Ramanan’s, I suppose. At the very least, when the party was in bad shape without seats and people were afraid to hold speeches, we still went down.

Not a single person here tonight was paid by me. I got a message when I came out earlier saying "we're doomed, there are only 30 people tonight, the hall isn't full." I said, "to hell with it," everyone is afraid because our party has become like that—people are afraid to attend. But I recognize the faces of those who are here because they have been everywhere since the *Reformasi* days, from demos, Bersih, to speeches where there were only five people. Once we became the government, they didn't get anything, but they believe we support this party for the good of the country.

If you want to defeat me or tell me to step down because I don't give money, don't hold an election, let's just hold a lottery. Whoever can give a bigger lottery, you become Deputy President. But if it is used as an excuse to hoodwink outsiders by saying the leadership is rejected by the grassroots on the grounds of not helping, this is a culture that we must fight. I have sat on the stage and spoken openly like this, I have reached the point of no return. I don't even care about winning or losing. We have built this party together for over 25 years. The threat is that if this culture is allowed to take root without being challenged, then what is our party worth in the eyes of the people? They will say we are just the same as those before.

We are doing this podcast not to show support, but because we need to save the party from this culture. I read those who just joined the party, the young ones, writing long essays—I thought "Jargon Man" was only in Bersatu, turns out there are some in PKR too. It’s so long, talking about *manhaj* (methodology), *wahana* (vehicle), and reformist idealism. Actually, how can you polish idealism if we ourselves know that the things we have fought against for decades have already started to show their seeds? If this matter is not fought, this party will eventually be rejected by the people. Be angry if you want to be angry that I am speaking openly; to me, it is better to be straightforward now than to hide and then have the people feel this is no longer the party they supported before. When we are punished in the election, regret is expensive, regret afterward is useless.

**Haziq Asfar:** You have explained many direct rebukes, YB. But you are the Deputy President, why not discuss it yourself with the President or in the MPP (Supreme Council) meeting?

**Rafizi Ramli:** In this situation—netizens always complain why is Rafizi not fierce when he becomes a minister like he was when he was the opposition? Friends at the branches also ask why the Deputy President is not vocal outside? We have to adjust to priorities. We joined the party to fight everything so that when Datuk Seri Anwar becomes Prime Minister and the party becomes the government, we can start making changes for the people.

In government, the process is long. There are those in the cabinet, there are chief ministers like Tok Mat. There is a process. In the party, what is actually happening in the Political Bureau or MPP? Of course, there are things I raise. I raise this culture to Datuk Seri Anwar and the MPP. Tok Mat is here, he knows I have raised it many times to be careful with the "madness" of branch chief elections when we become the government. I know that in some states, there are UMNO and PAS members who joined as members with e-KYC just to vote so their friends could win as branch chiefs.

I stated it in the meeting, but we just "took note" and expressed concern, but we have to realize that we are part of the Federal government. If we shake the party too much, the government will shake. The people do not need this problem; the people want us to focus on building the economy and helping them. Whatever can be solved inside, we solve inside. Perhaps my mistake was not being too firm earlier because I was "husnuzon" (thinking positively). Even though my friends and I mentioned this problem, when there is a call to "move on" so as not to disturb the government, the choice is for me to swallow it.

This is a lesson when becoming the government. I admit that in these two and a half years, I really had to balance between the image of government cohesion and my views on the party. The priority is to maintain government harmony so as not to give ammunition to the opposition. Whatever we can argue inside, we do, and in the end, we must respect the consensus or the President's urging. It’s just that now is the campaigning period, and everything that is happening, even if I don't say it openly, everyone already knows. Some people ask why waste time, better not to compete. My view is, this party is respected because of our objective culture. If there is disagreement, there will be parties that express it. We respect the process and the party's unity, but we never swallow everything we are fed whole.

If I don't compete, it's as if it confirms the general view that Keadilan has many "yes-men" and "sycophants." If I lose, it's okay. I was the Kemaman Branch Chief in 2014, won by only one vote. In 2018, I was unemployed, not yet free from prison, I just fought Azmin who at that time was a Senior Minister. Winning or losing is not the issue, but defending the party's foundation so it isn't seen as a "yes-men" party after gaining power, that is what is important.

**Haziq Asfar:** Your mission this time is to show we are not just a party of "yes-men." You don't have WhatsApp, but you have Twitter and Facebook. You might have seen the long jargon-filled essays by some branch chiefs or cabinet colleagues stating directly that they no longer support you as Deputy President. How about that, YB?

**Rafizi Ramli:** REFORMASI! REFORMASI! REFORMASI! Izy, I told you. People don't do things like that. Look, nobody picked it up directly. We are a party that cannot be centered on individuals. No need to mention the name Rafizi Ramli, no need to mention the name Nurul Izzah, no need to mention the name Anwar Ibrahim. Say "Reformasi," that is enough for our people. Can we?

**Audience:** Yes!

### **1. The True Meaning of Reformasi**
Rafizi emphasizes that "Reformasi" is not just a street slogan or a way to topple a leader, but a **structural and systemic change**. He acknowledges that the current phase (as the government) is much more difficult than the opposition phase because it involves implementing policies that are sometimes unpopular but necessary for the country's future.

### **2. Leadership and Government Challenges**
*   **Diesel Subsidy:** He defends the decision to retarget diesel subsidies. He states that the previous government knew this had to be done but lacked the political courage to implement it. Although bitter and affecting popularity, it is crucial to prevent leakages and strengthen the economy.
*   **PADU:** Rafizi explains that the PADU system is the backbone of fairer and more effective aid distribution, ensuring only those who qualify receive benefits.

### **3. History and Sacrifice in PKR**
*   Rafizi recalls the hardships of building the People's Justice Party (PKR) from scratch, where they had to sleep in the office, had no funds, and faced intense political pressure.
*   He emphasizes that the core values of a fighter are **loyalty and principles**, not just chasing positions.

### **4. "Influencer" Politics vs. Authentic Leaders**
*   Answering a question on the difference between grassroots leaders and social media influencers, he says the people are now wise enough to judge.
*   **Authentic** leaders are those who are consistent in word and deed, and have ideas tested through fieldwork, not just creating popular content on TikTok or Facebook.

### **5. Internal Party Issues and Upcoming Elections**
*   Regarding rumors of division or rejection by branch chiefs, Rafizi states he is not discouraged. To him, position is a trust and he is ready to work in any capacity, as long as the party's goals are achieved.
*   He criticizes the **"Airbnb Member"** phenomenon—members who only appear or are brought in during the election season for specific interests but are not actively serving the party.

### **6. Future Focus: Madani Economy**
*   Rafizi states his focus now is on the digital economy, Artificial Intelligence (AI), and ensuring Malaysia is not left behind in the global technological revolution.
*   He believes that by improving the economy, the people's well-being will be guaranteed in the long run.

### **7. Closing Message**
At the end of the session, when the audience chanted his name, Rafizi immediately corrected them and asked them to chant **"Reformasi"** instead. He stressed that this struggle is not about individuals (Anwar Ibrahim, Rafizi, or Nurul Izzah), but about the **reform agenda** for the people.

***

**Conclusion:** This podcast shows a pragmatic side of Rafizi Ramli who still holds onto his original idealism. He wants party members and the public to understand that building a nation requires difficult decisions and hard work that goes beyond political rhetoric.
