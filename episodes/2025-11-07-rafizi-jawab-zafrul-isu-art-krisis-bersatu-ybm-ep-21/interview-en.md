# Interview (English)

**Rafizi Ramli:** Assalamualaikum and welcome to the 21st episode of the podcast, together with YB Rafizi and...

**Speaker 2:** No more silly greetings this week.

**Rafizi Ramli:** None, none, YB. I have repented. But we have another special guest with us tonight—someone who is mysterious but has already been guessed correctly by...

**Speaker 2:** Well, you guys used his photo. Obviously people can guess. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** No, but the picture wasn't... it’s none other than...

**Speaker 2:** Put up a different picture, right, so it's a mystery.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Datuk Dr. Rais Husin, how are you, Datuk?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** Alhamdulillah. Thank you for having me.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Yes. So, Datuk Dr. Rais Husin is a former member of the Bersatu Supreme Council.

**Speaker 2:** What a way to start. Of all the things he’s done, you start with him being a "former" member. Allah.

**Rafizi Ramli:** No, that’s your caption. I’m just going along with it. If I may start, Doctor—you left Bersatu on 7 October 2022. You stated that one of the reasons for your resignation was health issues and other commitments. However, a few days later, you joined PKR. So, what was the real reason, Doctor? Was it your principles or something else?

**Speaker 2:** He asks a question, then gives a leading question and a leading answer.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** But that was indeed the health situation at the time. I was just recovering from a quadruple bypass in 2019, and I wasn't very active in politics anymore. My focus was primarily on writing, researching, and at that time, I was tasked at MDEC to clean up the system. It’s normal. Yes, that is what I was focusing on. As for politics, aside from commentary and op-eds, my TV appearances were quite restricted.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** But when I saw several things that were not aligned with what I first joined politics for... I was never a politician. In fact, Raf can always tell you, I always like to use the word: "I’m not a politician." I was always a technocrat—Telekom, IT, and so on. I joined in 2015 for the first time when Tan Sri Muhyiddin was sacked, and that was the time that interest in politics came about. But it was more on the administrative side—setting it up, the constitution, the logo, galvanizing support, strategy, and tactics. 

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** At that time, Tun Mahathir was reluctant to form a party. I said the only way to fight is to form a party, and the party must be a Malay-based party because any other party would be called *pengkhianat* [traitor]. There were five principles: Reform, Justice, Inclusiveness, Moderation, and Progressive. These were the core principles. I saw that things were moving away to become "real politics." At the same time, I was talking to Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim on the political front, but I said that politically, I didn’t want to be active. At that point in time, I saw these five principles as very congruent with PKR. So that’s why I joined. But fast forward to now—this is a strange story, and this is the first time I’m talking about it publicly, Raf.

**Speaker 2:** Okay.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** And I was never allowed to be active in the PKR party. I was quite amazed—I'm not saying that I'm the best or the good, but with all the experience that I have, surely I could contribute to the party. When I tried to ask why, the answer was very interesting—this came from a very senior PKR leader who is still in a position now. That person told me, "They are doubtful of you, Rais, because you were in Bersatu, you were fighting, you were Tun Mahathir’s man." I am always somebody’s man—either Tun Mahathir’s man, but for Tun Mahathir, I’m Anwar’s man. I find it strange when I become "another person's man." After all, I am Rais Husin. 

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** That’s why when I found that out, I didn't play any role in PKR, and until today, that is the truth. Because that was presented to me not by one senior leader, but three or four senior leaders who said, "You were too close to Tan Sri Muhyiddin, you were too close to Tun Mahathir, and therefore they were suspicious of you." *Astaghfirullahalazim*. I made a real move to join the party so I could play a role—not politically, meaning not so much for a position, power, or that sort of thing, but more of how I could contribute to the party. But that was put to a stop. This senior leader told me, not once or twice, but many times from different sources. I said, "Okay, I am fated to be somebody else's man."

**Rafizi Ramli:** Just one more thing before we start the segment. Doctor, you also worked with me during the transition agreement between Tun Mahathir and Datuk Seri Anwar. Could you brief us on that?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** Yes, that is the story of PH 1.0. At the time, I discussed it a lot with Iza as well. I remember at Pantai Hospital, while my wife was about to deliver our baby, we were at a coffee shop downstairs—the Zionist Starbucks. We spent about two hours discussing the way forward and making Tun Mahathir PM again. Why? Because I said that was the only way to get Datuk Seri Anwar out, to pardon the process. At that time, if you remember correctly, Raf, DAP didn't want to accept Tan Sri Muhyiddin as the PM candidate. But at that point in time, that was the only solution we had. The next day or the day after, Nurul and Iza went to London to discuss that. 

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** So, the transition agreement was 14 July 2017. I can’t forget it because my twins were born the next day, and that night until midnight, there was fighting at the PKR office. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** By everyone. And then Tun Mahathir said, "Look, if that is the case, we will leave." Salahuddin Ayub, if you remember, he got up.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** He said, "We can't do this." He was a bit... he said, "We need to do this." Then I asked, if you remember, "Can you give us a 15 or 30-minute break? We will come up with an agreement." Raf brought up an agreement, I brought up an agreement. And in that agreement, much of it focused on Datuk Seri Anwar. Truthfully, in my agreement, everything Raf mentioned—the transition and so on—was there, but we also focused a lot on the *Rakyat Malaysia*. Why? My view was that this agreement would be leaked. In Malaysia, everything gets leaked. So I knew it would be leaked. That is why we suggested that. Raf insisted on putting 24 months.

**Speaker 2:** You got 24 months, is it?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** Two years.

**Speaker 2:** No, no. To become the PM, 24 months.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Oh yeah, yeah. I insisted.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** Yes, you insisted. I forgot that. But I said no. If you do that...

**Speaker 2:** This is why it became open-ended.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** Let me explain, Raf. Because I said it would sit on the Prime Minister. At that time, remember, I’m not a politician, right? I really believe that everyone is in this together. I cannot "un-believe" that. The first one to sign the agreement was... the second one was... the third one was Tan Sri Muhyiddin, the fourth was Tan Kok Wai, and the fifth one was who I always call Ustaz Mat Sabu. The moment they signed, I wanted to leave for the hospital because my twins were about to arrive. Mukhriz said, "You hold on first until the press conference is finished." Once the press conference was finished, this time I didn't ask anyone, I just left. I was tiptoeing into Hospital Pantai at the time, and my wife said, "Oh, so you are here now? After all this, you are here now?" Good advice. The twins were born the next day, on a Saturday morning. Alhamdulillah. Otherwise, women are very good at history, you know?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** They won't forget or forgive me. That was the experience I had with Raf. We always had some positive interactions. We did a lot of predictions and so on. At that time, I was excited because it gave that "steroid" to believe "you are doing something for the nation." But after some time, you feel like you've been punched hard. Why? Because after they become PM or get into power, it's hard to access them because they are ring-fenced by certain people. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Okay, Doctor. But I was appointed as a Meranti [advisor] by Datuk Seri Anwar. 

**Speaker 2:** Yeah, right. Okay.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Right. Let's continue to the segment that YB Rafizi requested. 

**Speaker 2:** It is to help me talk less today. Otherwise, I’d be exhausted.

**Rafizi Ramli:** We will focus on ART and Bersatu. We will expedite the others. First, we have *Beria*.

**Speaker 2:** Beria. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Ah, *Beria*, man. 

**Speaker 2:** *Beria* here means "poyo" [arrogant/pretentious].

**Rafizi Ramli:** For old people, it’s *poyo*. Let’s see the first one.

**Speaker 2:** We have "using own children to promote fighting events." 

**Rafizi Ramli:** *Wira perasan* [Feeling like a hero]? 

**Speaker 2:** It appeared on my timeline, but I didn't follow it much. He was fighting with Jojo, right? This is about Muay Thai. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Muay Thai. Correct. I don’t know. 

**Speaker 2:** The rule of thumb is quite straightforward. You can't use minors or your own kids for this. In my own case, some people might like it if they become public figures, but my greatest challenge is to hide my wife and kids. They deserve a private life because this is our job. But our society is not careful about this. To me, this is crossing the line because it’s not a sensitive program—this is Muay Thai, where kids get hit in the face and bleed. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Go ahead, Doctor.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** I saw that on my timeline. I was shocked—not just surprised, but "gorilla-surprised"—because they are just kids. It’s too young to start this. In the context of today, where we see school bullying, celebrating gangsterism and fighting, maybe it’s not appropriate. I wouldn't allow my child to do this because it’s not something you want for your child. Playing football or badminton is fine, but that way... the Eastern civilization is lost. How do you nurture a child when you teach them to be like that? I was shocked. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Is this a warzone, or a children's Muay Thai championship?

**Speaker 2:** It’s mixed martial arts.

**Rafizi Ramli:** For what?

**Speaker 2:** For kids. There’s a children's category.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Society has to accept that parents need to be sensible. Whatever we do, it’s best not to involve children.

**Speaker 2:** And MMA, they really hit your face, it bleeds.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Right. *Beria* for your own sake. Next, YB? We have *Abah PM*. 

**Speaker 2:** This has to be asked to Datuk Rais. Do you still keep in touch with Tan Sri Muhyiddin?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** Very much. On a personal level, Tan Sri Muhyiddin is a very good man. I have no complaints about him. When he was PM, I had quite easy access to him. Alhamdulillah. During his premiership, there was an issue I couldn't support: the 5G Single Wholesale Network, which I believe is a scam. I went to see him a few times and told him, "It’s a scam, Tan Sri. Give me 15 minutes, and I will explain." 5G Single Wholesale Network was a failure in every country, except Brunei for their own peculiar reasons. I always advocated for a Multiple Wholesale Network. When Datuk Seri Anwar became PM, that was the only time I had a one-to-one session with him. Afterward, he was completely ring-fenced. Tan Sri Muhyiddin was willing to listen; he is a very calm and composed person. He doesn't easily lose his temper. I didn't agree with the takeover of the government—I pleaded on BFM that it shouldn't happen—but he is a good man.

**Rafizi Ramli:** So, what about this? Is he *beria* [eager] to be PM again?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** Please don't write him off. He is a very cool and composed person. Also, the data from Amara Research shows high accuracy in election outcomes. The numbers are showing that Perikatan Nasional should not be written off, especially in the Malay belt.

**Speaker 2:** So, *beria* here means we can't write him off.

**Rafizi Ramli:** It is appropriate for him to be *beria*. Datuk Rais says Tan Sri Muhyiddin has a chance to be PM.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** The numbers show that, unless the other side starts doing what they are supposed to do. 

**Speaker 2:** But PAS is not supporting him. 

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** PAS is clear that they want Datuk Seri Hamzah and not Tan Sri Muhyiddin. But PAS normally shifts their decision-making as required. If the person can deliver and provide stewardship, they will go with them. As for the present government, DAP is there, and they aren't going anywhere. But the rest of the unity government needs to do their job. Regarding UMNO, they keep saying they didn't sign with Pakatan Harapan; they formed the unity government at the behest of the King. My view is that a few months before GE16, UMNO will say, "Thank you for the time, we had a good ride, we are going on our own."

**Datuk Dr. Rais Husin:** I live in Bukit Jelutong, Shah Alam. The middle- and high-income Malays there have started saying, "This cannot go on." They expected more from the promised reforms, but things are just "two times five" [the same]. Politicians are like diapers—they need to be changed for the same reason. Do not rule him out. Sabah and Sarawak will follow the winner. With 165 parliamentary seats on that side, they will follow the winner. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** On the issue of "Flexi Mart"—if I may share my thoughts. Alhamdulillah. It adds a new headache; I get an anxiety attack every day. We have to check sales now. Economically, there are many things—government policies and the market, which is supply and demand. The conventional way to handle the cost of living has been for the government to give aid. That's fine. But if we don't manage the market, it's difficult. Why can't we control prices? Because if we do, it’s not a market economy anymore. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** When the government increases STR [aid], you are pumping money into the consumer market. Do we check if companies that get the windfall raise their prices? We don't. We are normalizing price increases. Society doesn't challenge a RM15 coffee or a RM15 lunch; we just accept it. The government responds by giving more money—taxpayer money. We must challenge the status quo. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Flexi Mart has been running for a week. The margins are very thin. A coffee that costs RM4 is sold for RM4.90. On paper, 90 sen is not bad. But thanks to the Malaysian public, we’ve had at least 400 people a day. That is a lot for a retail shop. But compare that to a typical shop where the cost is RM4 and they sell it for RM12; their margin is RM8. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Alhamdulillah, the traffic shows that if a shop offers low prices, it will get a response. And it unites Malaysians—PKR, Bersatu, PAS, UMNO, Chinese, Malay, Indian—everyone goes there. I need to prove that with these prices, it can still provide a 4-5% return per year to equity partners. Then they will see that instead of putting their money in the bank, it’s better to contribute to a social enterprise. You get a return that is comparable, but it helps the people. That theory is still holding up after a week.

**Rafizi Ramli:** It’s a challenge to the status quo. We are frustrated because for politics, people will fight to the death, but for the cost of goods, they don't want to challenge the status quo. We need to challenge it. I am positive that if the model is proven in one or two months, we can push for more. We want to prove that you don't need a government-style "Kedai Rakyat" model; it must be financially and operationally sound. It is efficient, operates on thin margins, and relies on volume. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** It has been a wonderful week for me. Malaysians can unite without us mixing in politics. I get comments saying, "I don't support YB, but I support this." I want to say, "You don't have to say you don't support me, just go to the shop." It’s a social experiment that exceeded my expectations. Affordability is the key. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** My late father had a shipping forwarding business and a retail shop. I was a businessman since I was 13—I sold Nasi Lemak and charged people to copy my assignments. My father's retail secret was simple: competitive sourcing. He went to KL almost every day to get things at wholesale prices, which widened his margin. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Regarding the 90-sen margin on coffee, that is fantastic. Others sell it for RM12 or RM13 because the coffee bean itself is only RM1. The rest is just overhead. So, others have 200-300% margins. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** As for the biggest group of customers at Flexi Mart in Shah Alam—we thought it would be students from UITM. No, it’s old Malay couples. Pensioners. They like to go there, buy coffee, and eat there. I am still studying why. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** Anyway, back to the Muay Thai issue. To me, this is crossing the line. Using children in such events is not appropriate. Celebrating fighting and bullying is not something you want for your child. How do you nurture a child when you teach them to be like that? I was shocked. Society has to accept that parents need to be sensible. Whatever we do, it’s best not to involve children. Especially when MMA involves hitting the face and bleeding. 

**Rafizi Ramli:** But anyway, it’s been a week of learning. It’s better than yesterday—though being better than yesterday doesn't necessarily mean it’s good yet. But to those watching, it’s been a wonderful week.
