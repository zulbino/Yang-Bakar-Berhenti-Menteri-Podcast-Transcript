# Interview (English)

**Aziq:** Assalamualaikum and welcome to the *Yang Berkhidmat* podcast, episode 21. Today we have another special, mystery guest, but one who was correctly guessed by our audience. We are with Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin. How are you, Datuk?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** Alhamdulillah, thank you for having me.

**Aziq:** Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin is a former Supreme Council Member of Bersatu. Datuk left Bersatu on October 7, 2022, citing health reasons and other duties, but just a few days later, Datuk joined PKR. What was the real reason behind this? Was it a matter of principle or were there other factors?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** At that time, I was indeed in the process of recovering from a quadruple bypass surgery in 2019, so I was no longer very active in politics. My focus was more on writing and research, and at the time I was given the task at MDEC to clean up and restructure the system. 

Politically, my commentary was limited to writing op-eds and TV appearances. But when I saw that several things were no longer aligned with my original intentions for entering politics, I began to reflect. I am not a hardcore politician—Rafizi knows this, I always say that I am a technocrat with a background in telecommunications and IT. I first entered politics in 2015 when Tan Sri Muhyiddin Yassin was sacked. From there, an interest grew to assist in terms of administration, constitutional registration, logo design, mobilizing support, and formulating strategy.

At the time, Tun Mahathir was quite reluctant to set up a new party. But I stressed that the only way to fight was to establish a Malay-based party, because any other party would continually be labeled as traitors. We held onto five core principles: reform, justice, inclusivity, moderation, and progressiveness. However, over time, the party shifted towards conventional partisan politics. 

At the same time, I had discussions with Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim. I emphasized that I did not want to be politically active holding party posts, but I saw that those five principles aligned very closely with PKR. Strangely, after joining PKR, I was never allowed to be active in the party. I found it puzzling—surely with the experience I have, I could contribute?

When I asked senior PKR leaders why that was the case, the answer was very interesting. A senior leader told me that they had doubts about me because I had previously been in Bersatu, fighting alongside Tan Sri Muhyiddin and Tun Mahathir. It was strange, because when I was with Tun Mahathir, people assumed I was Anwar's man! When I joined PKR, people assumed I was Muhyiddin's man. In reality, I am my own person—Rais Hussin. Because of that suspicion, I have not been given any major role in PKR to this day.

**Aziq:** Before we move on to the next segment, Datuk also worked together with YB Rafizi during the preparation of the transition agreement between Tun Mahathir and Datuk Seri Anwar. Could Datuk share that story?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** Yes, that was the story of Pakatan Harapan 1.0. At the time, I had many discussions with Nurul Izzah. I still remember sitting at Starbucks in Pantai Hospital—my wife was in the ward waiting to give birth to our twins—we spent nearly two hours discussing the best way to make Tun Mahathir Prime Minister again. That was the only way to secure Datuk Seri Anwar's release through the royal pardon process. At that point, DAP was not yet willing to accept Tan Sri Muhyiddin as the PM candidate.

The power transition agreement was dated July 14, 2017. The following day, my twins were born. That night, discussions at the PKR office were quite tense. Tun Mahathir even said, "If it's like this, we are leaving." The late Salahuddin Ayub stood up and insisted that we must find a resolution. I asked for a 15 to 30-minute recess for us to draft the agreement.

Rafizi brought his draft, and I brought mine. Rafizi's draft focused heavily on the pardon and the power transition for Datuk Seri Anwar. My draft encompassed those matters but also placed emphasis on the Malaysian public, because I knew this document would eventually leak to the public. Rafizi insisted on putting a specific timeframe of 24 months for the PM transition, whereas I suggested not putting a fixed timeframe.

**Rafizi Ramli:** I've almost forgotten the details myself...

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** Yes, Rafizi insisted on putting a two-year timeframe. I was concerned that if a fixed timeframe was set, the sitting Prime Minister would become a sitting duck. But Tun Mahathir, Kak Wan (Datuk Seri Wan Azizah), Tan Sri Muhyiddin, Tan Kok Wai, and Ustaz Mat Sabu ultimately signed the agreement. 

After the event concluded, I rushed straight to the hospital. That experience gave a boost of motivation that we were doing something for the country. However, after forming the government, even gaining access to leaders became difficult because they were fenced off by certain inner circles. 

Before joining PKR, I met Datuk Seri Anwar in Bukit Gasing. I stressed to him: "Datuk Seri, look after the people's welfare. The *perut economy* (bread-and-butter economy) is crucial." There are five fundamental areas: addressing the cost of living, affordable healthcare services, affordable housing, dignified and quality jobs, and economic stability. Over 90 percent of the people want these things. Yet the political cycle remains the same, and that sense of suspicion is still there.

**Aziq:** Alright, thank you, Datuk. Let's move on to our first segment, *Berekatan*.

**Rafizi Ramli:** This is a special segment designed to help me speak less today. Otherwise, people will tire of listening.

### Segment 1: Berekatan

**Aziq:** First issue: Using one's own children and minors to promote combat and fighting events like Muay Thai or MMA. What is YB Rafizi's view?

**Rafizi Ramli:** This issue keeps appearing on my timeline. The basic rule is quite simple: the involvement of underage children in promoting fights like this should not happen. Speaking for myself personally, my biggest challenge as a politician is maintaining the privacy of my wife and child, because they deserve a private life. 

Our society sometimes overlooks this aspect. If it's a regular program, that's fine, but this involves combat sports like Muay Thai. As parents, we must know the limits and not include young children in gimmicks like this.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** I was also shocked when I saw it on my timeline. The children are way too young. In the current climate where issues like bullying and gangsterism culture in schools are becoming increasingly concerning, things like this should not be promoted. I would never allow my children to be involved in something like this. Sports like football or badminton are fine, but combat sports showcasing bloody action and violence will erode civilization. How can we educate our children if this culture of brawling is normalized as a value?

**Aziq:** Second topic: "Abah PM" or Tan Sri Muhyiddin Yassin. How does Datuk view his current position?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** On a personal level, he is a good and calm person. When he was PM, I had good access to convey my views. There were issues I strongly opposed during his administration, for example, the implementation of the Single Wholesale Network (SWN) for 5G, which I considered impractical. I explained to him why the SWN model failed in other countries and recommended the Multiple Wholesale Network (MWN) model. He was willing to listen without flinching or overreacting. 

When Datuk Seri Anwar became PM, I also explained the same matter in a meeting at Parliament. The government eventually agreed to transition to a Dual Wholesale Network (DWN), although in terms of technical execution, there are differing views.

Regarding Tan Sri Muhyiddin's political standing, do not underestimate the capability of Perikatan Nasional (PN). EMIR Research's study during the last State Elections demonstrated a 95.6 percent accuracy rate. The data shows that voter support in the Malay belt for PN remains very solid.

**Rafizi Ramli:** But PAS seems disinclined to support Tan Sri Muhyiddin returning as Prime Minister. PN's strength lies in the Malay voters under PAS's influence. If PAS leans more towards Datuk Seri Hamzah Zainudin than Muhyiddin, how does Muhyiddin intend to navigate this?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** PAS can shift its stance according to the suitability of their political circumstances. News regarding PAS's disappointment with Muhyiddin mostly stems from rumors or individual statements. My experience with PAS is that they will support anyone who proves they can deliver results and provide leadership. 

For PH and the Unity Government, parties like PKR and UMNO need to execute what they ought to. UMNO has recently been repeatedly emphasizing that they formed the Unity Government on the decree of the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, not out of a formal bond with PH. It is not impossible that a few months before PRU16, UMNO might choose to go solo. If that happens, the contest will become wide open.

**Aziq:** Next, the issue of football fans placing stickers on an aircraft.

**Rafizi Ramli:** This involves the case of Ultras Negeri Sembilan placing stickers on the body of an aircraft and filming it for social media content. This is an unwise act and constitutes an offense.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** Sticking decals or stickers on public property or aircraft is an act of vandalism. If sticking decals today is tolerated, tomorrow or the next day people might go and spray-paint them.

**Aziq:** Next topic: "Raya is still far off, but there's a forgiveness session." This refers to Minister Nga Kor Ming's action in reprimanding a journalist, followed by holding a reconciliation and forgiveness session.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** I actually started out as a crime desk reporter at *The Sun* and *The Star*. During the Tun Mahathir era, ministers were considered extremely powerful. Today, with social media, every action of a minister is observed live. Nga Kor Ming's action in apologizing publicly was reasonable to defuse the situation, compared to some ministers who hang on to their egos too much.

**Rafizi Ramli:** I have a slightly different view. Spontaneous incidents like that actually reinforce perceptions and suspicion among Malay voters towards DAP. When a minister's initial reaction is to summon editors and target specific media outlets, voters see that as a manifestation of their true attitude. 

When an apology is subsequently made officially, the public views it as a superficial gimmick. Politicians cannot rely too heavily on gimmicks. Overly trying to appear friendly—such as reciting excessively forced *pantuns*—is often viewed negatively by fence-sitters. Things like this undermine Pakatan Harapan's efforts to build confidence among Malay voters.

**Aziq:** The final topic in this segment: Statements by PAS President, Datuk Seri Hadi Awang, using various animal metaphors to comment on political and international issues.

**Rafizi Ramli:** It was interesting reading that piece. More than 15 types of animals were mentioned—snakes, wild boars, deer, tapirs, tigers, cows, buffaloes, goats, crickets, cockroaches, grasshoppers, frogs, and rats. Datuk Seri Hadi has long enjoyed using animal metaphors in his writings.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** The analogies used regarding geopolitical relations carry a message, particularly regarding comparisons of economic and military scale. For example, militarily, the ship we recently received from the US was a 58-year-old used vessel. The underlying message is that a small nation like ours needs to be extremely cautious when navigating agreements with major powers.

### Segment 2: Standing Order (Parliamentary Questions)

**Aziq:** We move into the *Standing Order* segment. First question from the Pulai constituency to the Minister of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI): What is the ministry's strategy to ensure that the data center industry in Malaysia can utilize AI chips from both the United States (such as Nvidia) and China (such as Huawei Ascend) amid competing economic blocs?

**Rafizi Ramli:** This question is highly relevant to national technology policy. The digital world is currently split into two main tech stacks—one based on the Western bloc (US) and the other based on China (such as OpenAI vs. DeepSeek, or iOS vs. Android). 

As a small country that has not built its own foundational technology, Malaysia should remain technology agnostic—that is, not bound to any single technology ecosystem. If the trade agreements or tariffs we sign force us to rely solely on one tech stack (for instance, restricting the import of GPU chips from China), we tie down the country's freedom in the long run. Data center operators from various countries will be hesitant to invest here if their technology is not supported.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** I agree. Malaysia must practice active neutrality. We cannot restrict our technology options. Look at the example of the earlier 5G rollout, where there was a narrative that only one technology provider was the best, despite existing global alternatives like Huawei. 

When Huawei was blocked from using the Android operating system, within six months they successfully developed HarmonyOS. They made early preparations against the risks of GPU and AI sanctions. In the Malaysian context, if international agreements mandate that we must consult outside powers before making domestic tech decisions, it undermines national sovereignty. Why should we seek permission from foreign nations to determine the direction of our own technology investments?

Ministry responses are often general in nature, such as "ensuring Malaysia remains neutral" or through the *Silicon Vision* initiative. But in reality, the technology control policies between major powers require us to maintain an independent and firm stance of neutrality.

**Aziq:** Second question from the Pagoh constituency to the Minister of Agriculture and Food Security: What is the ministry's comprehensive strategy to address food inflation and prevent the "double burden" phenomenon (poverty and malnutrition) among the B40 group, who currently spend 71.9% of their monthly income on basic necessities compared to 64% in the T20 group?

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** This issue of food security is a very serious structural problem. Malaysia imports 73% of the country's chili needs, even though chili is the easiest crop to grow on our soil. The declared self-sufficiency rate for rice is around 60%, but the actual domestic production figure is estimated to be only around 48%, with the rest relying on imported supplies and other adjustments. 

Most concerning is seed self-sufficiency, where we import 83% of our agricultural seeds. Everything starts with the seed. If the value of the ringgit depreciates or if a global supply chain crisis occurs, the B40 group is the hardest hit. 

The classifications of B40 and M40 themselves are outdated. Due to the post-COVID economic crisis, we are actually seeing the emergence of a "B70" group because household expenditure on basic food items has nearly doubled.

**Rafizi Ramli:** UN reports on urban poverty in Kuala Lumpur also highlight the nutritional issues facing B40 children. Many poor urban families are forced to rely on instant noodles as a daily staple. This provides very low nutritional value and contributes to stunted growth, both physically and in terms of children's cognitive development.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** There must be efforts to support local innovation. For example in Sabah, a young entrepreneur produced instant noodles made from surplus mustard greens, which are nutritious. The government should provide funding and economies of scale to startups like this so that healthy nutritional products can be channeled to lower-income groups at affordable prices.

### Trade Agreements, Bersatu Issues, and Closing

**Rafizi Ramli:** Regarding the government's response to public concerns over trade agreements and tariff exemptions (ART): the government's explanatory approach cannot simply rely on a "trust me, bro" sentiment. 

Citizens and analysts read agreement texts word for word. When there are binding clauses or implicit advantages granted to outside parties, a minister's explanation that "we can manage it" is not convincing enough. The government's communication strategy needs to be more sophisticated and transparent in detailing the legal implications and long-term policies.

Regarding the internal crisis in Bersatu, the question arises as to why Datuk Seri Hamzah Zainudin's camp appears desperate for a swift leadership change before PRU16. Whether they feel PN's chances of winning are higher under new leadership, or whether there is an irreconcilable clash between Hamzah's group and Datuk Seri Azmin Ali's group. Tan Sri Muhyiddin might be seen as a protector to Azmin, prompting the Hamzah bloc to demand a clear transition timeline.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** The Statutory Declarations (SDs) mobilized by certain Bersatu MPs are actually aimed more at pushing Tan Sri Muhyiddin to set a clear transition plan, rather than demanding his immediate resignation. 

This style of internal politics is driven by behind-the-scenes maneuvering. Saifuddin Abdullah's statement on the party's financial issues also reflects the realities of managing an opposition party. During the early days of Bersatu's founding, leaders frequently used their own personal funds to manage the party. Politics cannot function solely on financial allocations.

**Aziq:** Our time has reached over two and a half hours. We managed to briefly touch on Zohran Mamdani's campaign in the US, which was popular for its music and rap-based social media approach.

**Rafizi Ramli:** His campaign style was very creative and relevant to young voters. Except that we here can't exactly start rapping like him.

**Aziq:** Alright YB, thank you very much. Thank you also to Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin for agreeing to come and share your deep insights tonight.

**Datuk Dr. Rais Hussin:** Thank you for inviting me.
