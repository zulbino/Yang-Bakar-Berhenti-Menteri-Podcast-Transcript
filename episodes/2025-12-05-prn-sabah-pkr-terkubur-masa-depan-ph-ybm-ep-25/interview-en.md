# Interview (English)

**Interviewer:** An asset with an original cost of $9,000 and accumulated depreciation of $7,000 was sold for $2,500. What is the gain or loss on the disposal of the asset?

**Rafizi Ramli:** To find the gain or loss on the disposal of the asset, we first need to calculate its book value. The book value is calculated as the original cost minus the accumulated depreciation:

* **Book Value** = $9,000 − $7,000 = $2,000

Next, we compare the sale price to the book value:

* **Gain/Loss** = Sale Price − Book Value
* **Gain/Loss** = $2,500 − $2,000 = +$500

Since the result is positive, it represents a $500 gain. Therefore, the correct option is **C. $500 gain**.

**Interviewer:** Next, consider an item of equipment with a cost of $25,000 and accumulated depreciation of $18,000 that is sold for $5,000. What will the entry to record the disposal of the equipment involve?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Let's calculate the book value and the gain or loss for this equipment first:

1. **Book Value** = $25,000 (Cost) − $18,000 (Accumulated Depreciation) = $7,000
2. **Gain/Loss** = $5,000 (Cash received) − $7,000 (Book Value) = -$2,000 (a $2,000 Loss on Disposal)

The complete journal entry to record this transaction is:

* **Debit:** Cash = $5,000
* **Debit:** Accumulated Depreciation = $18,000
* **Debit:** Loss on Disposal of Asset = $2,000
* **Credit:** Equipment = $25,000

Evaluating the choices against this journal entry:

* **A. A debit to accumulated depreciation of $18,000:** Correct.
* **B. A credit to equipment of $7,000:** Incorrect, because equipment must be credited for its full original cost of $25,000.
* **C. A credit to gain on disposal of $2,000:** Incorrect, as this transaction resulted in a loss, not a gain.
* **D. A debit to loss on disposal of $2,000:** Also correct.

Factually, both **A** and **D** are correct journal entries. In standard multiple-choice accounting test banks, **D** is frequently the primary tested concept to demonstrate recognition of the loss as a debit, though **A** is equally valid in accounting practice.
