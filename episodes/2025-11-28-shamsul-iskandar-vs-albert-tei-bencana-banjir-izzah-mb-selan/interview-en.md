# Interview (English)

**Speaker 1:** Assalamualaikum and welcome to episode 24 of the "Yang Berhenti Menteri" podcast. As usual, I am joined by Rafizi Ramli. YB, before we start our usual segments, I have a question from a listener. We are currently in the final week of the Sabah state election campaign. Some are asking: First, why isn’t YB Rafizi campaigning on the ground? Second, has any party requested your help?

**Rafizi Ramli:** The request was there, but I don’t feel that I add value. The reason is, it depends on the party machinery. Based on the last party elections, we know that the entire machinery and party leadership—the messages delivered, including everything that happened behind the scenes of those elections—paint a picture that leaders like me are not wanted. So, even if I go down, when the entire machinery consists of those who hate me more than they hate PAS, PN, or UMNO, it won’t help anyone.

There are many ways for us to contribute. I think many Sabahans are watching this podcast, so I am contributing by showing them that there is still a remnant of the reformasi spirit within PKR. Hopefully, their hearts are open to voting for PKR.

**Speaker 1:** Tomorrow?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Tomorrow, God willing.

**Speaker 1:** Very well, YB.

**Rafizi Ramli:** There is one more reason, since I’m already being asked. I have experience managing elections since 2011, starting with the 2011 Sarawak State Election. Another reason I feel there is no need for me to go is that voters in Sabah and Sarawak actually do not like it when people from the Peninsula or Peninsula-based leaders come over. That is a huge risk taken by PKR, especially because almost the entire campaign in Sabah is driven, led, and fronted by leaders considered "big figures" from the Peninsula. This actually reinforces the sentiment that PKR in Sabah is a Peninsular party suffering from a lack of local leadership—whereas they want locals.

So, it becomes a catch-22 situation. As I commented in previous episodes, bringing in these "party-hopping" candidates will weaken the morale and reputation of PKR at the grassroots level in Sabah. It gives the impression that Sabah PKR doesn't have great leadership and is forced to bring in outsiders, which will continue to erode confidence. At the same time, when the faces highlighted throughout the campaign are Nurul Izzah, Datuk Seri Anwar himself, and Saifuddin Nasution, if one is not careful, it adds to the feeling that Sabah PKR doesn't fully represent the aspirations of the Sabahan people and is just waiting for signals from the Peninsula.

That is why during the last party elections, I usually received good support from Sabah even though I didn’t give money or anything. I think what differentiates me from others in PKR is that I do not interfere. Sabah and Sarawak want to make their own decisions. So, let them. Unless their decisions appear to be different from the perspective of national interest, then we must present our arguments, but we cannot force them. That is my answer whenever candidates or the machinery ask me to go down.

**Speaker 1:** Did the candidates themselves ask?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Yes, candidates who are... good people. But they are all already great in their own right.

**Speaker 1:** While we're at it, I’m afraid I might be digressing, but it's fine, just a little bit. I read from the "Damai" group saying, "Even this KJ who was sacked is willing, his heart is big enough to go to Sabah to campaign. YB, who is still a Member of Parliament and a senior leader despite having no party position, isn't going." How do you want to deliver a message to the public?

**Rafizi Ramli:** I usually don't pay much attention; that is already "low level" stuff. If we want to measure contributions, those who complain and compare me to KJ were not even around when we were struggling. So, if we are counting contributions, I don’t feel I need to beat my chest and show that I have contributed the most. We were already there before PKR was even born—when the party lost, when we had to spend our own money, when we created the first internet radio from abroad.

That is what gave strength and built the party that exists today. It’s just that some of those who joined PKR in the last two or three years do not understand the history and the trials and tribulations of building this party, which is why they make such statements. Otherwise, I don't think the issue of who contributed more than KJ arises. I think if you asked KJ, even KJ himself would be embarrassed.

**Speaker 1:** Okay, fair enough, YB. Clear. Let’s move to the first segment. "Beria Mat." You were very enthusiastic in asking questions earlier, right? Okay, first one.

**Speaker 1:** This is the recent flood issue, YB. Someone crying because he got caught by his wife.

**Rafizi Ramli:** What’s this story? I don’t really follow social media stories, but even if it were the most viral one, it never appeared on my timeline. So, what’s the story?

**Speaker 1:** Farhan, you were very eager to explain earlier. How is it, Farhan?

**Farhan:** So, in the middle of the floods, there were reports on social media stating the husband said he was going to KL, but it turns out he got caught for a scandal there. Some also said it was a friend's husband.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Caught for a scandal there means suddenly he is in Hatyai with someone? Where did he tell his wife he was going? Did he say he was going to KL?

**Farhan:** Some say he really was meeting in KL, but his "side visit" was to Hatyai. Another one says he rode a motorcycle to Cameron, but ended up getting caught in Hatyai as well.

**Rafizi Ramli:** As far as I know YB, if you go to Hatyai, you don’t need to bring a scandal along either. (Laughs)

**Speaker 1:** YB, do you call it Hatyai or Hakyai?

**Rafizi Ramli:** People say Hatyai, but our people pronounce it Hakyai.

**Speaker 1:** Okay, Hatyai.

**Rafizi Ramli:** I just wanted to test who goes there often. Who can pronounce it correctly? Oh, even AMK (Youth Wing) didn't invite me. How could they. So what is the story?

**Speaker 1:** Eagerly lying to his wife. (Laughs) Usually, people who ride big bikes do go to Hatyai.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Why do people who ride big bikes go to Hatyai?

**Speaker 1:** Because it’s the first stop after crossing the border to the north of Thailand.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Where exactly is Hatyai? Is it on the west coast?

**Speaker 1:** If you cross Bukit Kayu Hitam, the first town is Danok, and it's about 60km after Danok. So, why is it so hard for us to go and rescue those who are flooding there?

**Speaker 1:** The United 6 Malaysia team has gone, and according to initial reports, there are indeed several Malaysian machinery teams that have gone in to help, but it was a bit late.

**Rafizi Ramli:** If you want to talk about being "enthusiastic" (beria), the ones who made up this story and made it go viral are also quite "enthusiastic." This issue is important because many people are stranded there and we need rescue as soon as possible. But when there are stories like this, it devalues the matter. People look at this Hatyai story as if it’s just a story about lying to a wife. Yet, how many people are stranded there? Hundreds or tens?

**Speaker 1:** It is said there are 4,000 Malaysians.

**Rafizi Ramli:** 4,000? So, I am more inclined to think those who made up this story are truly "enthusiastic" about nothing. Meanwhile, leave the actual story of the husband and wife for them to resolve. This story, how are we going to save the 4,000 people who are still stranded? Whose leg is that, by the way?

**Speaker 1:** Oh, this is his wife being furious, but the husband got caught cheating.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Crazy. Alright, okay. Very "beria," YB. (Laughs) A bit lacking. This is being "enthusiastic" about riding on other people's hardships.

**Speaker 1:** Okay, fine. Next, elections. "Citizens, please have an election every day." This video, what is he repairing here?

**Speaker 1:** Yes, one is fixing a bridge common to houses built over the sea. Another one here is cleaning.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Shafie Apdal?

**Speaker 1:** Yes, fixing a road.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Yes, that is why my view is that party machineries need to understand better that often it is the machinery that plans these gimmick programs.

**Speaker 1:** So the candidate has to go?

**Rafizi Ramli:** But many candidates like it too. For example, in the past, there was a minister who went down to wash a drain and so on. It is important for the candidate's officers or assistants to ensure the campaign program doesn't turn out like that. Otherwise, it will continue. The ones who will get hammered mercilessly are the candidates and these politicians. Part of the reason is our own society; the people around us and our political culture are just like that.

**Speaker 1:** Like gimmicks?

**Rafizi Ramli:** It’s not just that they like gimmicks, it’s "feudal." They feel, "Oh my God, look at this, he is an elected representative. If someone like this doesn't become a representative, what else?" If a representative does something good, "Oh my God, how great this representative is, he really loves the people." That is why I don’t like it when I see videos of Datuk Akmal (UMNO Youth Chief) or assemblymen going around scolding people. To me, that is very feudal. He works, I work. He has his job, I have my job.
