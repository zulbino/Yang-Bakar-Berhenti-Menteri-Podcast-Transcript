summary: In this episode of "Podcast Yang Berhenti Menteri," hosts Rafizi, Haziq
  and Zak are joined by Dr. Muhammad Irwan Arifin, an economics academic from Universiti
  Islam Antarabangsa Malaysia (UIA), to dissect Malaysia's 13th Malaysia Plan (RMK13).
  After a light-hearted introduction of Dr. Irwan, the conversation moves into how
  RMK13 differs from past five-year plans by focusing on root-cause structural reform
  rather than project lists — prioritising education, complexity of the economy, and
  talent development to escape the middle-income trap. They discuss brave but unpopular
  measures such as subsidy rationalisation, raising the retirement age toward 65,
  and starting school earlier while shortening its duration, framing these as necessary
  long-term sacrifices akin to intergenerational equity principles found in Islamic
  teaching. Rafizi explains the shift from "marketability" to "matching employment"
  as a graduate-employment KPI, and outlines structural economic weaknesses (dominance
  of small, low-value SMEs) that suppress wages despite GDP growth. The discussion
  turns to the "third sector" (philanthropy, wakaf, zakat) and Rafizi's idea to formally
  recognise and incentivise cash support that children give aging parents as part
  of the tax system, addressing looming ageing-population costs (Malaysia is projected
  to become an aged nation by 2044–2054). They compare Malaysia's trajectory to Japan
  and Korea's demographic struggles, debate cash versus in-kind child benefits to
  boost fertility, and highlight the fiscal glide path to reduce debt-service burden
  and deficit by 2028. Later topics include teacher professional development reform
  via IPGs, the role of ministers versus experts in policymaking, geopolitical risk
  from Trump-era tariffs (including new 100% chip tariffs) and their potential to
  derail RMK13's 3.5–4.5% growth assumptions, lessons from China's resilience under
  trade pressure, the GST-vs-SST debate (equity/regressivity and inflation concerns),
  the need for price transparency in supply chains, and the ambitious but framework-building
  timeline for introducing nuclear power (SMRs) by 2031. The episode closes with light
  banter about Rafizi's past visit troubles at UIA.
