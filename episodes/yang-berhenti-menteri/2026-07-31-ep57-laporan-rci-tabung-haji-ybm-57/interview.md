**Haziq:** You were among the very first figures to raise alarms about Tabung Haji back in late 2015, and you published extensive financial analyses on your website. After reading the newly released 252-page RCI report, how do you dissect its contents for the public?

**Rafizi:** To help viewers understand the report chapter by chapter, we must first establish the historical and political context. Politicians often change positions over time, forgetting that they leave a digital footprint. 

My involvement began in late 2015 when I was a Member of Parliament. In December 2015, the Governor of Bank Negara Malaysia, Tan Sri Zeti Akhtar Hassan, wrote a letter to Tabung Haji raising a major red flag: Tabung Haji’s financial reserves had fallen into negative territory. 

I raised this publicly and questioned Tabung Haji’s management. At the time, Tabung Haji’s CEO, Tan Sri Ismee Ismail, denied my claims and accused me of lying. I then examined Tabung Haji’s audited financial statements year by year. The latest available audited statement at the end of 2015 was for the financial year 2014; the 2015 statement was not yet out. 

Based on my projections, I concluded that the 2015 reserves would also be negative. This coincided with the 1MDB scandal breaking out, which caused a drop in the local stock market where Tabung Haji had significant equity exposure.

When Tabung Haji denied my projections, claiming they possessed substantial assets, I pointed out a key accounting issue: they were consolidating Bank Islam’s assets into Tabung Haji’s financial statements. Bank Islam’s assets are not liquid for Tabung Haji. Banks take deposits from the public, which constitute liabilities (*payables*). The bank uses those funds to invest and generate assets, but those assets must meet statutory reserve requirements because the bank owes that money to its depositors. Tabung Haji was pointing to Bank Islam's balance sheet to claim, "Look, we have plenty of assets." When I examined the figures line by line, I explained that the vast majority of those large assets belonged to Bank Islam and could not be liquidated to cover Tabung Haji's liabilities.

As they refused to acknowledge these realities, I demonstrated that the *hibah* (dividend) payouts were unsustainable and functioning like a Ponzi scheme—a point later affirmed in the RCI report. Eventually, I was charged in court.

When Pakatan Harapan assumed government in May 2018, steps were taken at the end of 2018 to rescue Tabung Haji through a financial bailout worth over RM30 billion. This led to the creation of Urusharta Jama'ah Sdn Bhd (UJSB). During that period, Najib Razak, Lim Sian See, and commentators from UMNO and PAS aggressively claimed that PH had plundered Tabung Haji's assets and auctioned them off to non-Malays.

What is ironic is that during the Parliamentary debates in mid-2019, the parties demanding an RCI were UMNO and PAS, while Datuk Seri Mujahid Yusof Rawa argued that an RCI was unnecessary. Today, Mujahid asks for credit, while Perikatan Nasional claims the RCI was established under their administration to show they saved Tabung Haji. 

The Royal Commission consisted of highly respected figures, including former Chief Justice Tun Raus Sharif, Tan Sri Rashid Hussain, and former Chief Secretary to the Government Tan Sri Samsudin Osman. The RCI report was completed around August 2022. When I first read it, I was pleased because as a former accounting student, it felt like receiving an exam score six years later confirming that my original financial analyses and projections from 2015 and 2016 were accurate.

Regarding my court charges, by late 2018, the government was fully aware that my disclosures were factual, and the restructuring was already underway. During my trial in 2019, Tabung Haji's main witness, former CFO Datuk Rozaida Abdul Rahim, was herself subject to an internal Disciplinary Inquiry recommending her dismissal. Realizing the prosecution had no case and that proceeding would only further expose Tabung Haji's internal failures, the Attorney General's Chambers withdrew the charges against me.

The RCI report confirms that the early political narrative pushed by BN and PN—that my disclosures were lies and that PH destroyed Tabung Haji—was false. However, PH supporters should not celebrate blindly either. If they read the report carefully, they would realize it is a double-edged sword that highlights ongoing risks for the current administration.

**Haziq:** How did the Royal Commission conduct its investigation, and what are the core focus areas of the report?

**Rafizi:** The Commissioners faced constraints of time—they had six months—and limited staffing, supported by officers from the Attorney General’s Chambers. Rather than conducting an exhaustive criminal investigation under the Penal Code or MACC Act, they relied on top-level documentation, review reports by three consulting firms (EY, PwC, and Roland Berger), official financial statements, and witness testimonies. 

The report focuses on four primary operational issues:
1. **Financial Reserves:** Determining whether Tabung Haji’s reserves were genuinely negative.
2. **Hibah (Dividend) Practices:** Assessing whether past dividend payouts complied with the law and were financially sustainable.
3. **Investment Losses:** Evaluating bad investments and impairment losses.
4. **Hajj Subsidies:** Examining the escalating, unsustainable financial strain of muassasah hajj subsidies.

Beyond these four operational issues, the report addresses root causes: political interference, weak corporate governance, executive bonuses awarded during deficit years, and systemic business model flaws. 

Crucially, the report also uncovers issues regarding Shariah compliance and zakat. It reveals that during certain periods—specifically under Najib Razak's administration—the continuous shifting of deposit contract structures (*akad*) from *mudharabah* to *wadi'ah yad dhamanah* meant that Tabung Haji stopped functioning under a true profit-sharing model. Under *wadi'ah*, Tabung Haji became a borrower of funds, meaning zakat paid by Tabung Haji only covered its corporate entity, leaving depositors technically exposed to unpaid personal zakat obligations on their savings. Additionally, the report notes instances of investments in non-Shariah-compliant equities.

**Haziq:** Let us examine the first core area: the negative reserves and the 2018 bailout.

**Rafizi:** The RCI confirms that Tabung Haji’s financial reserves were negative continuously from 2014 through 2018. 

To understand why a rescue was necessary in late 2018, we must look at the timeline. The first Cabinet meeting on the restructuring took place on December 7, 2018. By December 14, the Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV), Urusharta Jama'ah Sdn Bhd (UJSB), was incorporated. By December 19, the asset transfer framework was set, and on December 27, the Asset Transfer Agreement was signed. A transaction involving tens of billions of ringgit was finalized in three weeks.

Why the rush? Under Section 22 of the Tabung Haji Act 1995, Tabung Haji is legally prohibited from paying *hibah* (dividends) if its assets are less than its liabilities (i.e., when reserves are negative). Following the change of government in May 2018, the incoming administration could not legally declare a dividend for 2018 without rectifying the balance sheet. Had no dividend been declared, political opponents would have organized massive street protests claiming Tabung Haji was ruined under a non-Malay-led coalition. 

To satisfy the legal requirement, Tabung Haji’s assets had to exceed its liabilities. Tabung Haji holds liabilities primarily in the form of depositor savings—which stood at around RM88 billion—plus debts and sukuk. Its assets consisted of cash, real estate, subsidiary shares like Bank Islam, listed equities, and unlisted equities.

Reserves become negative when the value of assets drops below total liabilities. This occurs when assets are purchased at inflated prices, when stock values collapse (such as FGV), or when subsidiaries suffer continuous losses requiring ongoing cash injections.

Under International and Malaysian Financial Reporting Standards (MFRS/FRS 136), companies must conduct annual impairment reviews and mark assets to market value. If Tabung Haji bought FGV shares at over RM4.00 and the market price dropped to RM2.00, the RM2.00 difference per share must be recognized as an impairment loss in the profit and loss statement, reducing the overall asset value on the balance sheet.

By 2018, after applying proper impairment rules, Tabung Haji held RM10.9 billion in net book value of non-performing, toxic assets. These were assets that yielded no returns or suffered heavy capital losses. Because these assets could not be sold on the open market for their book value, the government created UJSB—a direct subsidiary of the Ministry of Finance—to take these toxic assets off Tabung Haji’s balance sheet.

In exchange for transferring RM10.9 billion worth of toxic assets to UJSB, Tabung Haji needed to receive valuable consideration. Because the government did not have RM10.9 billion in cash to buy the assets outright—and national debt was approaching the statutory ceiling of 55%—UJSB issued zero-coupon sukuk (government-backed debt paper) to Tabung Haji.

To restore Tabung Haji’s reserves to a positive state, the financial instrument issued could not merely match the RM10.9 billion book value of the toxic assets. The government had to issue sukuk with an overall redemption value far higher. The overall government financial obligation through UJSB sukuk up to maturity (spanning 2019 to 2029) amounted to RM27.5 billion—and with additional financial commitments, the total bailout cost approached RM30 billion.

This paper transaction replaced toxic equities on Tabung Haji's balance sheet with a high-value government paper asset (Sukuk UJSB). This artificially made total assets exceed liabilities, enabling Tabung Haji to legally declare a modest *hibah* of 1.25% for 2018 and continue paying dividends in subsequent years.

Furthermore, removing RM10.9 billion in assets deprived Tabung Haji of potential dividend income from those holdings. To compensate for this income loss, the UJSB sukuk structure included an annual "deferred payment for yield" element set at approximately 4% per annum. This allowed Tabung Haji to recognize roughly RM800 million to RM900 million in paper revenue every year from the sukuk, which was factored into its annual reported profits. 

However, that annual RM800 million to RM900 million revenue was purely an accounting entry on paper—no actual cash was transferred into Tabung Haji's bank accounts.

**Haziq:** Was there a better alternative available to the government at the end of 2018?

**Rafizi:** The RCI evaluated four alternative recovery models submitted to the government at the time, including direct cash injections or immediate asset liquidations. The RCI concluded that given the fiscal constraints and legal parameters in late 2018, the government's chosen restructuring model was the correct step, subject to key execution caveats.

**Haziq:** Let us move to the second core area: *hibah* practices and what you termed the "PakMan Telo" or Ponzi scheme dynamic.

**Rafizi:** Under the Tabung Haji Act, *hibah* can only be distributed out of genuine realized profits after ensuring positive reserves. However, starting around 2010 and peaking between 2014 and 2017 under Najib Razak's administration, Tabung Haji declared exceptionally high *hibah* rates—6.25% annual plus 2% hajj *hibah* in 2014 (totaling 8.25%), 8% in 2015, 5.75% in 2016, and 6.25% in 2017.

In reality, from 2014 onwards, Tabung Haji’s reserves were already negative. Realized returns from the stock market and bonds were averaging only 4% to 5%. To pay out RM2 billion to RM3 billion annually in *hibah* without underlying profits, Tabung Haji used fresh capital deposited by new depositors to pay "returns" to existing depositors. That is the definition of a Ponzi scheme.

**Pa'an:** How was this legally and operationally possible without triggering institutional safeguards?

**Rafizi:** The legal safeguard existed in the Tabung Haji Act, which prohibited dividend distributions during reserve deficits. To bypass this safeguard, Tabung Haji management engaged in what the RCI delicately describes as "creative accounting."

Tabung Haji’s former CFO, Datuk Rozaida, argued before the RCI that the Tabung Haji Act did not explicitly specify adherence to standard accounting frameworks. Consequently, management ignored Malaysian Financial Reporting Standards (MFRS 136) regarding market impairment. Instead, they manufactured an internal pro-forma metric called *Realizable Asset Value* (RAV).

Under standard MFRS rules, if an asset’s market value drops, the loss must be recognized immediately in the profit and loss statement. Under Tabung Haji's internal RAV framework, management altered accounting policies to stipulate that an asset would not be impaired unless its market value fell by an extreme threshold—initially set at 70%, then later adjusted to 10% over extended periods.

By switching to this custom RAV accounting policy twice in 2017, Tabung Haji magically transformed a real financial loss of RM1.4 billion into an artificial paper profit of RM3.4 billion, creating a swing of nearly RM4.8 billion. This paper profit was then used to justify paying out high *hibah* rates prior to the 14th General Election.

The RCI report strongly criticizes the Auditor-General (Ketua Audit Negara) at the time. Despite these accounting manipulations, the Auditor-General failed to issue a "qualified opinion" (a formal audit warning) on Tabung Haji's 2017 financial statements, issuing only a mild "emphasis of matter" note. The RCI recommends that future audits of Tabung Haji be conducted by independent professional audit firms rather than the Auditor-General.

**Haziq:** You mentioned earlier that the report also highlights Shariah and zakat compliance issues during this period.

**Rafizi:** Yes. The report notes that in 2016, Tabung Haji unilaterally altered the deposit contract structure from *mudharabah* (profit-sharing) to *wadi'ah yad dhamanah* (guaranteed custody/savings). 

Under *mudharabah*, Tabung Haji was obligated to distribute profits according to an agreed ratio. However, management began using investment profits to fund heavy hajj subsidies without explicit consent from depositors. 

To bypass this restriction, they changed the contract to *wadi'ah*. Under *wadi'ah*, Tabung Haji legally became a borrower of depositor funds. Consequently, zakat paid by Tabung Haji as an institution only covered its own corporate liabilities, not the underlying wealth of the depositors. Depositors were left unknowingly responsible for paying their own personal zakat on those savings. In December 2019, Tabung Haji rectified this by adopting the *wakalah* (agency) contract model.

**Haziq:** Let us address the third core area: investment losses. Critics like Dr. Akmal Saleh have asked, "Where is the *sakau* [corruption/theft] in the RCI report?" Can you detail the specific investment failures identified?

**Rafizi:** The RCI report details numerous problematic investments between 2014 and 2020 that resulted in massive financial destruction:

1. **PT TH Indo Plantations:** An Indonesian subsidiary involved in selling 83,000 hectares of land in Riau to PT Borneo Pacific. The agreed sale price of USD 910 million was arbitrarily reduced by USD 100 million (approximately RM400 million). Furthermore, Tabung Haji transferred share ownership to the buyer before receiving full payment and absurdly advanced RM178.6 million in loans to the buyer to cover obligations the buyer was supposed to pay.
2. **Emas Kiara Industries Berhad:** Tabung Haji acquired a 15.3% equity stake for RM20.1 million in June 2016. A put option was executed in April 2017 requiring the vendor (Lingkaran Hartaniaga Sdn Bhd) to buy back the shares after a failed IPO listing. To date, only RM2 million of the RM20.3 million put option has been recovered, forcing a write-down.
3. **Deru Semangat Sdn Bhd:** Tabung Haji acquired a 55% stake from the late Sultan of Pahang for RM231 million and committed RM295 million for estate development. TH ran out of funds to fulfill its commitments, forcing an impairment down to RM32 million before a restructuring agreement was reached with the Regent of Pahang.
4. **Trurich Resources Sdn Bhd:** A 50:50 joint venture established in 2009 with FGV to develop 200,000 hectares of palm oil estates in Kalimantan. The venture failed, rendering Trurich insolvent with net liabilities. Tabung Haji was forced to write off RM364 million in impairments, while Trurich defaulted on an outstanding USD 179 million (approx. RM700 million) loan from Maybank. Total losses from Trurich exceeded RM1 billion.
5. **Putrajaya Perdana Berhad (PPB):** In December 2014, Tabung Haji acquired a 30% stake in PPB for RM193.5 million from Cendana Destini Sdn Bhd. PPB was originally acquired by Jho Low using 1MDB/PetroSaudi funds in 2011. Its subsidiary, Putra Perdana Development (PPD), was part of the money trail that received RM140 million from SRC International. Cendana Destini bought PPB from Jho Low using a RM210 million bank loan, and shortly after, Tabung Haji bought 30% of the company for RM193.5 million. By December 2020, Tabung Haji recorded an impairment loss of RM145.3 million on PPB, leaving its net book value at just RM48 million.
6. **Other Failed Investments:** The report lists heavy losses in TH Plantations, TH Properties, TH Hotel & Residences, Alam Maritim, TH Marine, and FGV (where TH lost over RM1 billion in share value). Older failed ventures prior to 2014 include Silverbird (RM90 million lost), TH Heavy Engineering (THHE), and Perisai Petroleum.

To Dr. Akmal Saleh and UMNO leaders asking where the *sakau* is: the RCI explicitly states on page 138 (paragraph 3.14.1) that it found **"suspicious transactions and concealment of information."** 

An RCI is not a criminal prosecution body. Its role is high-level fact-finding. When billions of ringgit disappear through arbitrary price discounts, failed put options, and inflated acquisitions, those funds flow through brokers, proxies, and round-tripping accounts—exactly like 1MDB. 

This is why the RCI recommends full **forensic accounting investigations** and specialized task forces (similar to MACC's original SRC team) to trace the money trails and hold board members, executives, and politicians accountable.

**Haziq:** Which political figures and cabinet ministers bear responsibility for these failures?

**Rafizi:** Ultimate oversight rested with the Prime Minister and Finance Minister at the time, Najib Razak, along with Second Finance Ministers Datuk Seri Ahmad Husni Hanadzlah and Datuk Seri Johari Ghani. Ministerial oversight of Tabung Haji fell under Minister in the Prime Minister's Department Datuk Seri Jamil Khir Baharom and his Deputy, Datuk Dr. Asyraf Wajdi Dusuki.

Decisions on *hibah* payouts and executive bonuses—which reached up to 11 months of salary during deficit years—required Finance Ministry approval. While Jamil Khir appeared out of his depth administratively during Parliamentary debates, figures like Asyraf Wajdi—who holds a PhD in Islamic Finance—repeatedly publicly dismissed warnings of reserves deficits as "fitnah" (slander).

**Haziq:** Let us turn to the present and future. What is the current financial standing of Tabung Haji under the Madani government, and how does it align with the RCI's recommendations?

**Rafizi:** Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim recently stated that the RCI report was held back until Tabung Haji's balance sheet became strong enough to avoid public panic. However, an analysis of Tabung Haji's recent audited financial statements (2022 versus 2025) reveals persistent structural risks.

First, let us examine depositor cash flows:
* In **2022** (before the current administration took full effect), new deposits totaled **RM23 billion**, while total withdrawals were **RM21 billion**, resulting in a **positive net cash inflow of +RM2 billion**.
* By **2025**, new deposits dropped to **RM20.2 billion**, while withdrawals rose to **RM21.4 billion**, resulting in a **net cash outflow of -RM1.3 billion**.

Second, let us examine asset quality on the balance sheet:
* Out of Tabung Haji's total assets of **RM101 billion** in 2025, **RM24.78 billion (approx. 25%)** consists entirely of UJSB paper sukuk.
* Out of Tabung Haji's total group revenue of **RM5.34 billion** in 2025, **RM971 million (approx. 18%)** is paper revenue accrued from the UJSB sukuk. No actual cash flows into Tabung Haji for that RM971 million; it is an accounting accrual.

This leads to the critical issue regarding the **UJSB Sukuk Maturity in May 2026**.

The 2018 UJSB restructuring was designed as a temporary "Band-Aid." Under the original agreement approved by Cabinet in April 2019, the government agreed to allocate funding annually (approx. RM1.73 billion per year under RMK-12 and RMK-13) so that UJSB could redeem the First Tranche of Sukuk in cash upon maturity in May 2026. The principal and accrued yield redemption value for Series 1 in May 2026 stood at **RM13.2 billion**.

Had the government paid RM13.2 billion in cash in May 2026, Tabung Haji's paper assets would have converted into real liquid cash, permanently fixing its balance sheet.

Instead, when May 2026 arrived, the government did not redeem the RM13.2 billion sukuk in cash. Instead, the government transferred two pieces of land—a 0.63-hectare plot in TRX and 8,800 hectares of plantation land in Sarawak—valued combined at **RM970 million**. 

For the remaining **RM10 billion-plus balance**, the government simply **rolled over the debt into a new 10-year paper sukuk** maturing in 2036 at a 3.825% coupon.

While the new sukuk carries a government guarantee and a fixed coupon, the underlying reality remains unchanged: **RM10 billion in paper assets was simply replaced with another 10 years of paper debt.** The structural cash hole was kicked down the road once again.

**Pa'an:** How does the escalating cost of hajj subsidies factor into this long-term risk?

**Rafizi:** The RCI report explicitly warns that the current model of hajj subsidies is financially unsustainable. 

By **2030**, annual hajj subsidies are projected to reach **RM742 million**, with subsidies covering **60%** of the actual cost per muassasah pilgrim (while the pilgrim pays only 40%, or around RM12,000). To support this level of subsidy without depleting capital, Tabung Haji’s total fund size must maintain a massive threshold (around RM80 billion to RM88 billion) generating high returns.

Currently, 75% of total deposits in Tabung Haji are held by just 5% of top depositors—individuals using Tabung Haji as a high-yield, tax-free, government-guaranteed investment vehicle rather than a hajj savings fund. When high yield expectations force management to generate unsustainable returns, it creates pressure for creative accounting.

If hajj subsidies consume RM740 million annually, it shaves roughly 0.4% to 1.0% off the annual *hibah* rate distributed to all depositors. The government must transition to targeted hajj subsidies reserved exclusively for the truly needy and elderly, while allowing lower, sustainable baseline dividend expectations (4% to 5%) focused on capital preservation.

**Haziq:** What are your final conclusions and recommendations moving forward?

**Rafizi:** First, the narrative that Pakatan Harapan "sold off" Tabung Haji's assets in 2018 is completely debunked by the RCI. The report confirms that only one parcel of land was sold, and 75 out of 106 equity counters were liquidated under normal portfolio management, with Tabung Haji retaining first right of refusal on all UJSB assets.

Second, the public and law enforcement agencies—including MACC and the police—must maintain pressure to ensure full forensic accounting investigations are carried out on the specific transactions highlighted in the report. Those responsible for deliberate financial mismanagement must be prosecuted.

Third, political leaders across all parties must stop treating national trust funds as political leverage. Rolling over RM10 billion in paper debt for another decade does not solve Tabung Haji's structural deficit—it merely defers the crisis to future generations. Until the government fulfills its cash obligations to redeem the UJSB sukuk and reforms the hajj subsidy framework, Tabung Haji is not yet fully out of the woods.
