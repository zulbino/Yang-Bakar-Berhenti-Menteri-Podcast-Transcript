# Interview (English)

**Host:** Assalamualaikum and peace be upon you. Welcome to the 37th episode of the *Yang Beri Menteri* podcast with our brother, Rafizi. We have many issues to cover today, and our regular guest is back with us.

**Rafizi:** You’ll be the one arrested by the MACC later, you’ll be finished. [laughter]

**Host:** What did I do wrong? Okay, let’s move to the first segment. The new Chairman of the BBC (Backbenchers Club) did not receive a job description. Is this because she reprimanded components of the Madani Government to stay disciplined? Perhaps the public wants to know the actual role of the BBC chairman, YB.

**Rafizi:** I think the public doesn't quite understand what the BBC is and what its role is. If someone receives the highest number of votes from Members of Parliament, it means they are very eager (*beria*). When she issued that statement, she was referring to two groups. First, my group, which has a different view, that the Bill on the separation of the Attorney General and the Public Prosecutor needs to be improved. But more dangerously, she gave comments and reminders to stay disciplined to DAP.

**Host:** Oh, yes, there was a response to DAP.

**Rafizi:** Yes, she responded to DAP. Actually, the matter of inter-party relations within the government is above the paygrade of the BBC Chairman. That matter is managed at the PH Presidential Council level for PKR, Amanah, and DAP, or at the Unity Government Council level for relations between BN, PH, and GPS. So, I guess Zaliha hasn’t received her job description yet; she thinks her duty is to reprimand and discipline everyone in Parliament. Usually, the BBC looks after the welfare of MPs and acts as an intermediary between the MPs and the government. That doesn't really touch on policy either, because there is already a Special Select Committee (JKP) for every ministry. So, she is very eager to be the BBC chairman; she’s like a school prefect waiting at the gate.

**Host:** It’s only been 10 minutes and she’s already taking down names.

**Rafizi:** No, I’m not a prefect, I’m the one who always gets the cane. A bad boy. That’s why I’m very sensitive to prefects who take down names; it’s too fast. Just wait a while. Speaking of prefects, we have another prefect coming up after this. I think the image of PKR now is a school prefect image, eh?

**Host:** That’s true, we didn’t think of that. There’s Zaliha, there’s Fuziah. Ah, Fuziah Salleh is now the Secretary-General of DAP? Question mark.

**Rafizi:** This is because she "jumped the gun." But it hasn't reached that level yet; there’s just a hint of it. My understanding is that this stems from DAP’s statement that they would hold a vote to determine whether to remain in the cabinet and government positions or not. But suddenly, Fuziah issued a statement as if she were speaking on behalf of DAP. DAP’s promise to continue supporting the Unity Government is important for stability, but I think even DAP is saying, "What is with these prefects from PKR?" Prefects in parliament, prefects in the party, and now she is speaking on behalf of DAP. It’s the same as in Sabah; she speaks on behalf of GRS.

**Host:** Okay, fair enough. The third instance of being "eager" (*beria*). Dr. Akmal hasn't resigned yet, has he?

**Rafizi:** As far as I know, not yet. He declared it but it wasn't accepted, because the process of the many meetings he speaks of takes too long. Does he symbolize Malaysian bureaucracy? Yes, the bureaucracy to resign is just that long. I hope that after this, if I’m declared to have left PKR and am no longer an MP, he is still stuck in the bureaucratic process.

**Host:** He hasn't declared it yet. That's embarrassing, isn't it?

**Rafizi:** This follows DAP saying, "he goes, or we go." Then the UMNO Youth Chief said there’s no need to pretend to threaten the PMX; if you’re brave, leave now. So, coming from Dr. Akmal, he is indeed very "eager." As long as he doesn't step down as EXCO, I advise Dr. Akmal not to go near matters of resigning or leaving the government. Ultimately, I don't understand why there was a need for Dr. Akmal to comment on this? He was very "eager" to comment. If, for example, DAP had directed those comments at UMNO, we would understand why the UMNO Youth Chief would need to react. But why is he so "eager" to enter the ring? Is there any "tea," YB?

**Host:** Is there "tea"? I don't understand. Is there an "inside story"? That’s Gen Z slang.

**Rafizi:** I don’t drink tea, I drink coffee.

**Host:** Okay, let’s be a bit "eager" then. We move to the last one, the new PN Chairman. GDP doesn't matter, as long as you're happy. This is about the Setiu district, Terengganu.

**Rafizi:** I think he wants to say that the measure of economic growth isn't everything. But we have other measures like the Well-being Index or the Gini coefficient. Out of all of them, he chose the happiness index. He is very "eager" to justify why Terengganu is among the lowest-performing states. If the justification is that GDP doesn't matter as long as we are happy, we might as well all move to Bhutan. If we look at Maslow’s hierarchy of needs, as we move up, the things that make us happy vary. When at the very bottom, we only care about food and shelter. But when we move up, we think about education and cars, until we reach the top level related to intellect and principles. If a state leader, let alone a future Prime Minister, holds the view that it's okay for the people to be poor as long as they are happy, that is quite dangerous. As leaders, we have a huge responsibility. The message from the potential PN PM is that it's okay to go to university and become a Grab driver, it's okay to struggle, as long as you are happy. So if you want to be happy, just give them happiness medicine, give them a pill every day.

**Host:** Are you confident, YB? Look at their faces, you're confident, right?

**Rafizi:** You wouldn't give it to the prefect on duty.

**Host:** Okay, fine. Before we go to "Hidup Keras," there is one standing order. YB received an answer to your question to the Minister of Education. Your question was regarding educational benchmarking in Southeast Asia.

**Rafizi:** I asked because findings show that Malaysia is at the bottom in terms of total learning hours for STEM subjects. We are worse than Vietnam, Myanmar, and the Philippines. We only have 576 hours, whereas other countries like Cambodia—imagine, we are losing to Cambodia—have about 1,000 hours. I asked why Malaysia is at the bottom and what the solutions are.

**Host:** And the answer?

**Rafizi:** The answer is long, do you want to read it? Simply put, before MBMMBI, it was 3.5 hours a week. Then, because of MBMMBI, it was shortened to 3 hours, and by 2027, it will be increased back to 3.5 hours. That’s it. They didn't answer the question. They felt they were answering the question, but they missed the mark. We asked how to go from 576 hours to 1,000 hours, and they told a story about how it used to be 3.5 hours, then went down to 3 hours. The funny part is, the increase back to 3.5 hours is only an addition of 32 hours, leading to 608 hours. That is still far from 1,000 hours. Fadzlina, every time I ask about education issues, says, "Wait, we will announce it in 2027." As if 2027 will solve everything. Goodness. In reality, it’s only increasing to 608 hours, which doesn’t close the gap at all.

**Host:** A broader perspective, YB?

**Rafizi:** I understand if Members of Parliament feel frustrated. The questions asked aren't just for show; they are data-driven. But there is a tendency for ministries to consider MPs' questions as trivial and a nuisance. There are categories of ministers who feel important and answer "this is a government secret," some who talk in circles without answering, and some who just play dumb when asked for data. This is what the "prefect" Zaliha needs to look at as BBC Chairman. The quality of answers from ministries must meet the requirements of the original question. If not, it’s not fair. We want MPs to have a surge, to play an effective check-and-balance role. But when they get subpar answers that don’t meet their needs, and MPs are forced to accept them because there are no house rules that allow them to intervene easily, eventually parliament becomes a "rubber stamp." In the end, what is always reported is just the shouting, while matters like this are actually quite important. I am disappointed with that answer.
