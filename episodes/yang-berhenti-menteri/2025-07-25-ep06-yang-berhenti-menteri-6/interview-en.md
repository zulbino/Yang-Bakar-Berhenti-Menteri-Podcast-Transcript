Dynamics of power depend heavily on character, approach, and internal political movement. Why did the PKR election turn out the way it did? Because our view was that major decisions must be consulted and decided collectively, rather than unilaterally by the President or Prime Minister—even if legally within their executive scope, like the appointment of Tan Sri Azam Baki. Because it carries huge implications for PKR, PH, and the government, it conveys an impression that it was agreed upon, when in fact it was not discussed.

**Zaim:** Why does that mindset and culture exist? Isn't it shaped by the underlying system and its leaders?

**Rafizi:** You can design the most perfect system, but if the people operating within it do not fundamentally believe in its principles, they will always find loopholes. If a Prime Minister installs systems without changing societal values, corruption persists. If society rewards shortcuts to quick wealth—whether through politics or speculative schemes—over decades of hard work, people will pursue shortcuts. That is a cultural issue.

If the leadership lacks moral authority, you cannot transform the culture.
