**Host:** Right, 'Life Is Hard, Dik'. For the first hard issue, I think this week it's about BUDI95. Even though this has been explained several times, I want to ask a little bit about it. Regarding BUDI95, which is 300 liters for everyone. YB has spoken at length about the factors that determine and influence it. But *what if*, after this, the price of crude oil *per barrel* increases like it did a few years ago, and at the same time, there are problems with the USD and Ringgit Malaysia value? **Rafizi:** This is a comment I have made before. Meaning, I have been asked repeatedly, especially by the media, about my views on the government's action and decision to give 300 liters to everyone regardless of income limits. Previously, the discussion at the government level and the views of economists were to move from blanket subsidies to targeted subsidies. Targeted means only those who are eligible should receive assistance, and that eligibility cannot escape income limits. There is a reason why economists or market analysts state that targeted subsidies must follow income limits and not be applied across the board to everyone. This is because the blanket subsidy system that exists now—where everyone gets it regardless of income—exposes us to the risk of market price fluctuations.

For example, based on the statement from the Second Minister of Finance, Datuk Seri Amir Hamzah, the savings are estimated to be around RM2.5 to RM4 billion a year. I am sure that calculation is based on current oil prices, which are around 67 to 68 dollars a barrel, at an exchange rate of around RM4.21 to RM4.22 per dollar. Generally, the *rule of thumb* is that for every one-dollar increase in the price of world crude oil, the price of RON95 should rise by about 3 to 4 sen. The actual calculation is more complex because it is based on the supply and demand of RON95 petrol, as well as the price published in Singapore through a publication known as MOPS 95.

If following the APM (*Automatic Pricing Mechanism*) formula, a 1-dollar increase in crude oil would raise the RON95 price by 3 sen. The current price is around 67 to 68 dollars. In 2023 and 2024, the average world crude oil price exceeded 80 dollars a barrel. In fact, if I recall, the crude oil price used in the 2023 and 2024 Budget projections was around 85 dollars a barrel—which is a difference of around 20 dollars compared to the current price. That 20-dollar difference means there is an impact of a 60-sen increase.

The actual price of RON95 without subsidy is around RM2.60 to RM2.70 per liter. Therefore, the government has to bear a subsidy of 60 to 65 sen for every liter of the 300 liters given to everyone. Imagine if world oil prices rise again to the 80 dollars per barrel level, or if the Ringgit weakens to RM4.40. The subsidy for every liter of the 300 liters used by the people regardless of income limits will increase from 65 sen per liter to RM1.30 to RM1.40 per liter. When the subsidy reaches that rate, the total government subsidy bill risks exceeding the RM20 billion mark again. Previously, the country’s annual total subsidy was around RM20 to RM25 billion, and that is the main reason we want to implement subsidy retargeting.

With savings of only around RM2.5 to RM4 billion, if a rise in world oil prices occurs that forces the government to bear RM1.30 to RM1.40 per liter to keep the selling price at the RM1.99 level, the RON95 subsidy cost borne by the government will return to the original value of around RM20 billion. Ultimately, at that time, we will ask again: what is the point of us working so hard to do this subsidy retargeting if the situation hasn't changed at all?

World crude oil prices have been at those high levels. The current level is considered low because world economic projections are expected to weaken and economic growth is slower for the next two or three years. However, if the world economy grows again like in 2022, 2023, and 2024, crude oil prices will surely return to above the 80 dollars per barrel level. If that happens, what is the point of subsidy targeting?

The main goal of this RON95 subsidy retargeting has two points. First, to strengthen the country’s fiscal position so that we get savings of around RM8 to RM10 billion every year. Second, to limit the government’s *exposure* to world crude oil price volatility, which complicates the country's budget planning. If we implement subsidy retargeting correctly, the government’s exposure might be limited to around RM10 to RM12 billion a year, no matter how world oil prices fluctuate. But when it is done this way, that exposure remains open. So, the goal of reducing the government’s exposure to crude oil price *volatility* will not be achieved.

What’s more unfortunate is that if the subsidy cost returns to RM20 to RM25 billion like before, the people will complain. Even after going through the trouble of registering using an identity card, the oil price from RM1.99 might have to be raised back to RM2.30 because the government can no longer afford to bear the high cost. The people will question why previously they could enjoy a price of RM2.05 per liter without having to use an identity card, without a 300-liter limit, but now they have to face the same dilemma when world oil prices rise sharply.

Secondly, I have read a report from the Political Secretary to the Minister of Finance, Kamil Munim, who stated that this is only the first phase where 300 liters are given to everyone regardless of income limits. He stated that in future phases, the T15 group might no longer be eligible and would have to pay the full market price. Giving 300 liters and then withdrawing it later will only trigger new polemics and controversy between the government and the people.

That is why my stance from the beginning was to *bite the bullet*. If you want to implement it, do it right. Don't take an approach that is too diplomatic or try to balance everything to protect votes until it drifts from the original goal. Our task in the government is to make difficult decisions. I have always been confident that if we explain this policy well, curb corruption, and channel assistance directly to the people without leakage, the people will understand and appreciate the difficult decisions made for the good of those who are truly eligible.

This is not a matter of me being dissatisfied, but it is my responsibility as a Member of Parliament to voice this matter so that the government is always cautious. If the goals of RON95 subsidy retargeting are not fully achieved, the original problem we are trying to solve will surely return to haunt us, especially when world crude oil prices spike again. In the next 6 to 12 months, we will get the full picture of whether this current method is truly effective in helping the country or otherwise.
