  as Minister of Economy and Deputy President of PKR after losing the party election.
  Rafizi explains that his resignation was a matter of principle rather than sulking
  ("merajuk"), arguing that losing his party mandate meant he had also lost the Prime
  Minister's confidence, which would have made him a "lame duck minister" unable to
  effectively implement policy and would have dragged his ministry down with him.
  He clarifies the distinction between rakyat's mandate (given to MPs) and ministerial
  appointments (made by the PM), rebuts accusations of not being a "team player" by
  citing his career record at Petronas and in PKR/INVOKE, and defends his right to
  speak freely outside Cabinet due to collective responsibility constraints. He lists
  numerous policy achievements from his tenure at the Ministry of Economy (labour
  market reform, energy transition, fiscal reforms, GovTech, new growth engines) and
  argues these will continue under his successors. He rules out forming a new political
  party, citing the importance of political stability, and says it is premature to
  discuss the 2028 PKR election. He introduces the concept of a "third sector" (beyond
  public and private sectors) as a future focus, promising more details later, and
  states his plan going forward is to return to grassroots engagement as a backbencher/ordinary
  MP.
