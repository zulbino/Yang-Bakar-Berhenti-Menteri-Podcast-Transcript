**Haziq:** Kena jawab sikit demi sikitlah, YB. Saya rasa pemilihan pun dah habis dan macam-macam yang berlaku. YB dah resign, jadi orang-orang bertanyakan hal ini.

**Haziq:** We want to make a change, YB. Whatever the decision, YB has a platform to make a change as a minister. Why don't you just continue? Why do you have to resign? In people's eyes, they understand that a minister has the capacity and can do that—maybe some people think there's even a magic wand. But why did YB choose to resign?

**Rafizi:** Saya ingat tak ramai orang pernah jadi menteri. Saya pun sebelum jadi menteri ada pandangan yang berbeza tentang proses dalam kerajaan. Bukannya kalau seseorang itu jadi menteri, dia boleh buat keputusan seorang-seorang dan benda itu terus berlaku.

There are decisions that involve basic policy decisions. So when the policy decision is made, it must be brought to the cabinet and it must get approval from other ministries. Then after the decision is obtained, it must be implemented. So actually, kalau kita nak bawa perubahan, maksudnya kita kena mengubah dasar, dan dasar itu perlu mendapat persetujuan semua.

In this government, the political dynamics are still there. If someone wants to change the status quo—plus if I am the Minister of Economy, which involves the policies of all ministries and complicated things—then the political dynamics are quite important.

If we look at the example that happened after the election in Keadilan, apabila Nurul Izzah bertanding, everyone knows that it was with the blessing of the President of the party, who is also the Prime Minister. When the Prime Minister gives his blessing for his candidate to compete in the presidential election, the indication is quite clear that the President of the party, who is also the Prime Minister, has lost confidence in me as a candidate, as the Deputy President, and also as the Minister of Economy.

So when the message is clear, it will be translated into the government that the Prime Minister has lost confidence in my position in the party and also as the Minister of Economy. And when we look at the election decision, especially the decision where those closely aligned with me were cut off, it gave an image to the government, civil servants, and other ministers that actually I and my team have been rejected by the party.

Jadi bila you sudah hilang kepercayaan daripada Perdana Menteri, kemudian you hilang mandat daripada parti, kalau tiba-tiba you nak terus melakukan perubahan besar yang memerlukan sokongan dan support daripada kementerian-kementerian lain dan penjawat awam, menteri lain akan kata, "Buat apa aku nak goncang, susah-payah untuk orang yang dah hilang keyakinan?" So, what for? Jadi akhirnya, kalau saya kekal, saya akan jadi *lame duck* minister.

Dan sebagai menteri yang dilihat sudah hilang keyakinan daripada Perdana Menteri sendiri dan daripada parti dia sendiri, kalau saya terus nak buat kerja yang bagi saya ada kesan kepada negara, dia akan mengheret kementerian saya. Penjawat awam di dalam kementerian saya kena buat macam-macam, susah payah, akhirnya ditolak. Kecualilah aku nak jadi "Menteri Pasu Bunga" [ornamental minister] yang masuk *clock in*, *clock out*, hanya makan gaji buta tapi tak ada kesan. *And that's not me.* Saya juga kena fikir tentang fungsi dan peranan Kementerian Ekonomi itu supaya tidak terheret di dalam perkara ini.

It's quite different between private companies and the government, because the government needs legitimacy to make changes. So if the message is clear, through this election, that the President and the Prime Minister have lost confidence in me, then for kepentingan negara, adalah lebih baik untuk berundur secara terhormat.

**Haziq:** YB, saya nak *try* sikit. Tadi YB dah cakap tentang perubahan *and it's not* macam *magic stick* menteri buat PADU terus jadi. Dan YB pun dalam *posting* YB ada cakap bahawa YB dah tak terikat dengan rahsia, jadi saya cuba tanyalah. Contohnya pasal PADU...

**Rafizi:** Tu jawab kemudianlah, nanti tak pasal-pasal satu malam kita cerita pasal benda *technical* macam itu. Tengok dia cuba. Aku senang, aku buli je moderator.

**Haziq:** Okay, baik-baik. Kita pergi kepada apa yang orang katakan seterusnya, YB. Ni lebih kepada pandangan penyokong pro-pemerintah. Dia kata, setiap kali pemilihan ada yang menang, ada yang kalah. Biasalah yang kalah itu dirangkul, dan PM pun dah pujuk, tapi kenapa tak makan saman pujukan ini?

**Rafizi:** Pujuk, macam saya sebut tadilah, dia bukan soal pujuk tak pujuk. Saya tak merasakan ada kebaikan kepada negara kalau you jadi minister *lame duck* yang akan mengheret kementerian you sendiri kerana mesej dan isyarat itu jelas.

Saya ingat orang yang mengatur semua ini pun tak fikir panjang. Sebab itu semasa kempen saya kata, sepatutnya perkara ini dibincangkan dan difikirkan panjang-panjang. Kenapa perlu mengheret kerajaan dan negara? Kerana bila ada goncangan dalam parti seperti ini, dia akan ada kesan. *I think it's very naive to think that* perancangan itu tidak akan membawa kesan sekurang-kurangnya kepada parti.

Bila reaksi daripada rakyat dan anggota parti itu negatif—sekarang dah lebih kurang dua bulan dan masih belum reda—tiba-tiba pimpinan parti kena turun ke setiap negeri untuk redakan semua. Mereka yang melawan saya sudah tentu terpaksa membawa naratif baru. Untuk menggantikan saya, mereka perlukan alasan yang kukuh.

*So, I think the reason is difficult, so it's just a personal attack. That's what the story is about—to show that I was actually persuaded, because to be persuaded, there must be a reason. But I think that's an afterthought; it's not really based on the initial intention.*

So the question of being persuaded... *I also find it strange that the word "pujuk" is a response to the word "merajuk".* Kalau kita lihat maksud *merajuk*, *merajuk* is when people don't get something. Kalau anak kita nak baju, kita tak bagi, dia *merajuk*. Kita bagi baju, baru dia berhenti *merajuk*. My case is different. Saya memang menteri, dan saya tak mahu jawatan menteri itu. Jadi tak timbul soal merajuk atau apa.

Akhirnya dia kembali kepada soal prinsip tadi. Soal letak jawatan ini mesti bersandarkan kepada isu yang lebih besar: kita mahukan pimpinan negara yang berada di dalam kabinet mesti bebas untuk menyatakan pandangan, terutamanya kalau kerajaan itu menjanjikan perubahan dan reformasi.

*Reformations, if you want to do something—whether it's law reformation or economic reformation—it's controversial. It will change the status quo, so every single thing we want to change will definitely bring a difference of opinion.*

So if I were persuaded to go back, it means when the election in the party took place, I only had two options. One is that I stay in the government, right? But staying in the government, given the situation, comes with a condition: shut your mouth, sit quietly, because you are a minister who has no mandate.

Adakah mereka di luar yang bertanya, "Tak payah merajuk, buat kerjalah, jadilah menteri"? Dulu kita juga yang nak tukar kerajaan kerana kita nak menteri-menteri yang akan bersuara dan menegur. Kalau tidak, kita pernah melalui zaman 1MDB dulu—menteri semua "ya, ya, ya" sahaja, akhirnya negara jadi macam itu. Kita dah melalui proses yang panjang itu. Jadi saya tidak mahu menjadi anggota kabinet kalau secara tersiratnya pandangan saya dikekang atau saya tidak boleh mempunyai pandangan yang berbeza.

Dan saya juga tak mahu, atas dasar "jangan merajuk, buat kerja untuk parti", usaha saya melambungkan PKR tetapi oleh kerana saya tidak ada ruang untuk berbeza pandangan, maka saya tidak ada pengaruh *or can affect the decision about who gets elected, who deserves what, or stop the bad political culture that I don't agree with.*

Finally, if I'm not careful, I will become an enabler to everything that I was against before PKR became a government. Kalau saya kata saya tak suka budaya bodek, kerana itu sebab kenapa PKR wujud. *Because we don't want a culture that is a mess.* That's what Dato' Seri Anwar said many times. But if I insist, that culture is getting more rooted, so that people who we actually know are not worthy get places just because they brought a bag and bodek.

So I'm being asked to remain as a minister, or offered a role in the President's office to continue doing work, so that I become "flower and makeup" to attract people who agree with me to support PKR, but behind it all, I don't have any say in decisions. That is a fairly important principle for me. If we are really sure of reformation, then kita kena pastikan bahawa ruang itu ada sepenuhnya. Kalau tidak, sampai bila-bila, setiap kali ada orang yang berbeza pandangan dalam parti, mudahlah tuduh dia merajuk. Masa aku buat kerja separuh mati dulu, tak timbul pula cerita merajuk.

Masa contohnya saya bongkarkan pasal 1MDB daripada awal, saya satu-satunya pimpinan parti daripada PKR yang masuk keluar kampung bertahun-tahun *to expose and bring people's understanding of 1MDB. PKR got a big boost from that. Even when Dato' Seri Anwar first mentioned 1MDB in Parliament, the one who wrote the statement was me.* At that time, the question of elections didn't arise. But when I have a different view—especially when criticizing and expressing concern about the culture of *bodek* rooted in the party—suddenly I'm accused of being upset.

So that's why, for me, the question of being upset or going back goes back to the basic question. I'm not a minister, it's okay. I don't have a position, it's okay. But the things I mentioned earlier are for the sake of the future and the continuity of PKR. And if PKR is now a government leader and wants to be a group that impacts the politics of the country for years to come, then these things are important and cannot be ignored just like that.

If that matter is resolved, whether I'm in the party leadership or government or not, it's actually better. Because those who get support from the people are supported not because of our individuals, but because of the ideas, culture, and political values that we bring—which, in the last election, mendapat banyak kritikan daripada orang ramai.

**Haziq:** Agak membimbangkan bila YB kata tak boleh bercakap itu dan ini. Tapi YB, naratif "merajuk" ini sebenarnya adalah hujah utama sebahagian penyokong. Pada rakyat yang mungkin tak dapat konteks penuh, logik mereka mudah: YB dipilih melalui pilihan raya di P100 Pandan, menang, dan jadi menteri. Kemudian YB kalah dalam satu *internal election* yang tidak melibatkan keseluruhan rakyat—hanya peratusan kecil sahaja yang terlibat. Jadi timbul pandangan kononnya meletak jawatan itu mengkhianati mandat rakyat. Ini pandangan awam yang tidak mengikuti pemilihan parti secara dekat.

**Rafizi:** Kita kena buat kelas pendidikan politik yang lebih panjanglah. Dulu-dulu, *there was civic education at school.* Bila ada *civic education*, biasanya bila dah lulus Tingkatan 5, orang faham sistem kerajaan. Tapi sekarang saya rasa generasi anda tidak melalui *civic education*, jadi mereka menyamakan kerja dengan syarikat dan kerja dengan kerajaan. Majikannya berbeza.

Kerja syarikat memanglah ada majikan, kita tandatangan kontrak, kena buat kerja. Dah makan gaji, buatlah kerja sampai selesai. Menteri ini lain, kerana rakyat hanya memilih ahli parlimen. Daripada ahli parlimen, kalau ahli parlimen dari satu parti itu cukup syarat Perlembagaan, maka ketua partinya boleh membentuk kerajaan dan menjadi Perdana Menteri. Yang melantik menteri itu bukan rakyat; yang melantik menteri adalah Perdana Menteri.

Jadi sebab itu saya kata, kita berbalik kepada prinsipnya: kalau yang melantik itu memberi isyarat halus bahawa dia tidak bersetuju dengan beberapa pandangan saya, atau dia mahu menguruskan pentadbiran kerajaan dengan cara yang berbeza daripada pandangan saya, jadi kalau saya kekal pun, prinsipnya sama ada saya perlu mengubah cara saya ataupun saya terpaksa bersetuju dengan perkara yang saya tidak setuju.

Kalau kita balik kepada mandat rakyat pun, sebenarnya rakyat memilih kita berdasarkan janji kempen *or the value that we give to the people so that they appoint us as Members of Parliament. The mandate of the people is actually to hold on to that principle.* Sebab itu orang tak selesa kalau menteri yang sudah ditolak oleh parti masih nak terhegeh-hegeh jadi menteri. Mandat daripada rakyat adalah berdasarkan tugas dan prinsip semasa anda dipilih.

*Then you are appointed by the Prime Minister. So that is a matter at the government level; it is not related to the rakyat appointing the Prime Minister.*

Kalau boleh saya simpulkan lagi, rakyat mahu menteri bekerja, tetapi menteri perlukan sokongan menteri-menteri lain dan penjawat awam. *Other ministers and civil servants actually read the political signal. If the political signal means he has lost his mandate from his party and lost the trust of the Prime Minister,* maka apa-apa yang saya nak buat pun akan diperlekehkan. Akhirnya dia akan mengheret kementerian, dan harapan rakyat supaya Menteri Ekonomi boleh buat kerja tidak dapat dilaksanakan.

*Therefore, it is reasonable for the Prime Minister to appoint an Economic Minister who has his mandate and confidence so that he can direct other ministers and signal to civil servants. Only then will the government be able to function and carry out things as planned.*

**Haziq:** I think that makes sense. Prime Minister adalah ketua parti yang melantik menteri, manakala rakyat hanya memilih ahli parlimen. Jadi untuk katakan meletak jawatan sebagai menteri itu mengkhianati mandat rakyat, *it's not related at all.*

Okay YB, hari ini kita nak cakap pasal apa yang orang kata. Saya nak balik kepada hujah penyokong pro-pemerintah tadi...

**Rafizi:** Why are you always busy with them?

**Haziq:** Tak boleh, YB, sebab nanti orang sebut juga...

**Rafizi:** Ini masalahnya, moderator pun dah terpengaruh dengan cybertrooper ini.

**Haziq:** *We have a lot of big things.* Tapi okay, saya rasa YB dah jawab soalan itu banyak kali. Ada satu lagi tohmahan: "not a team player". Dalam mana-mana organisasi, apabila sesuatu itu berjaya, ia adalah kerana kerjasama berpasukan. Kalau tak setuju dengan itu dan ini, dianggap *not a team player*. Macam mana nak tadbir? Betul tak?

**Rafizi:** Itulah, ni berapa puluh minit menjawab geng-geng empat lima orang sahaja yang bising dekat TikTok dan Twitter. Mudah untuk menyerang peribadi dan buat *character assassination*.

Orang yang dah lama jadi ahli politik macam saya sebenarnya tak ambil pot pun orang cakap apa. *Basically, your record should be your best defense; your record should speak for yourself.* In my case, people who make up stories and attack individuals can say whatever they want, but I've gone through a wonderful career for almost 26 years.

Kalau saya bukan *team player*—sebab ada kata PM tak suka sebab saya bukan *team player*, kononnya saya dengar pendapat sendiri sahaja—sepatutnya saya kerja kat mana-mana pun kena buang. *But that's not the case. I had a wonderful career.* Contohnya kat Petronas, saya pengurus paling muda dilantik pada umur 25 tahun—umur kau berapa sekarang?

**Haziq:** 27.

**Rafizi:** Aku masa umur 25 dah jadi ketua unit, dah jadi pengurus. Saya jadi Ketua Perancangan Perniagaan untuk perniagaan Petrokimia Petronas pada umur 27 tahun. Kalau saya bukan *team player*, tak adalah saya naik pangkat sebegitu cepat.

Dalam politik pun, tahun 2016 masa kita gerakkan INVOKE sampai PKR menang 47 kerusi dalam PRU 2018, kempen kita melibatkan sukarelawan seramai 40,000 lebih. *These are volunteers. Do you think it's easy for us to gather volunteers, to motivate them without paying them, and to arrange them to campaign systematically? If I had a problem working in a team, how could I manage 40,000-plus volunteers who had no prior connection with PKR?*

So I think all of these are personal attacks that we don't need to layan. What is most important is how we want to move on.

For me, the issue of being a team player... one of the reasons why I think the good decision is to withdraw from the cabinet is precisely because if you lose the mandate, you will drag your whole ministry with you because you become a *lame duck* minister. I don't want to be a minister who only takes wages.

*It is very important for us to be balanced in the government. If you think that a matter will be accepted in a different way by the people, then it is your duty to speak up.* Finally, the decision will be the decision of the cabinet. People give their views, but you must be taken seriously within the cabinet, because your view represents pandangan di luar sana. Kalau tidak, the extreme of a "team player" is agreeing to everything. Tak naklah *team player* macam itu, nanti masuk gaung.

Kita selalu melawak atau perli penyokong PAS—"walaun". Bila semua *walak* kononnya *team player*, walaupun keputusan jelas tak betul. Ambil contoh pasal 1MDB: sebelum PAS keluar dari Pakatan Rakyat (2011–2014), saya berkempen pasal 1MDB. Yang paling banyak telefon suruh datang ceramah pasal 1MDB ialah PAS. *But suddenly, because of political decisions made by PAS leadership who wanted to be good to UMNO, PAS leaders went to listen to Arul Kanda's explanation, and they came out saying they were satisfied.* Penyokong kat bawah pun satisfied sebab kononnya *team player*. That is a sure way to cause all the political problems we have now.

Jadi *team player* kena dalam konteks yang betul. Kalau saya menguruskan sesuatu, saya tak mahu dikelilingi orang yang mengangguk dan *yes-man* sahaja. *Later people will say "the emperor's new clothes"—meaning the king wears no clothes, but everyone says his clothes are beautiful.* It cannot be like that.

*To me, if I want to do my job well, I must be surrounded by competent people.* Orang kompeten bukan sahaja cekap, tapi ada keberanian, integriti, dan nilai untuk menegur dan berkata, "Saya tak setuju, kalau buat begini akan jadi begitu." *As a leader, I want my team to be like that. To me, that is a true team player, not everyone saying "yeah, yeah, yeah".*

Every time the "team player" accusation comes out, I get worried. I've been in this party longer than most leaders at the front now. The party was never like that before. It never questioned differences of opinion by saying "oh, you're not a team player". What kind of party do you want to have? And if PKR is the backbone because the Prime Minister comes from PKR, what happens if you bring that culture into the government? It's even worse.

So yeah, we don't need to layan all these pro-government attacks. *View* aku pun lagi banyak daripada mereka kat sosial media.

**Haziq:** YB mentioned earlier a view regarding team players. In politics, if someone dissents from the direction, it can be tough. But with YB's experience, if someone disagrees with YB, is it welcomed for added value to get the work done?

**Rafizi:** It's actually like this. If we are in a small team to do a program in a parliamentary constituency, everyone has a view—this person wants water, that person wants it done in front of his house, another says the neighbor is useless. Allow everyone to give their views.

Dalam proses itu, masing-masing mestilah ada hujah dan fakta yang betul. Pada masa yang sama, dia ada *track record* yang baik dan kita tahu kalau dia kata sesuatu, itu sebab dia ada pengalaman dan *competency to carry it through.*

Akhirnya, the person chairing the meeting—like me in Pandan—listens to all kinds of stories. It's like choosing food: this one wants curry puffs, that one wants something else. After hearing all of it through rich discussion where everyone is allowed to speak, you assign weightage. Some people have a good track record, and usually the things they say are true, so you then make a decision.

Tapi bila naik ke atas—contohnya masa saya jadi Naib Presiden dahulu—kalau ada benda yang saya dah suarakan *and that has been discussed, and I don't agree with that view, the buck doesn't stop at me because I'm just a Vice President.* There are two or three people above me, so the buck stops at them. They will have to carry through the decision and accept the effects.

But in my case, bila saya Timbalan Presiden, *the buck stops at me and also the President.* When it reaches a level where, despite my track record and contributions, there is no room for my views to be considered, then the only honorable thing for me to do is to withdraw.

**Haziq:** YB, *are you serious in getting the job done?* Sebab YB dah bagi macam-macam formula, kemudian YB *resign*.

**Rafizi:** Yes, I get a lot of messages and emails saying, "Oh, you have to stay to get the job done." First, at the risk of sounding repetitive, the dynamics of government are very important to understand. Untuk melakukan kerja memperbaharui ekonomi, itulah sebabnya mandat sangat penting.

*Because the mandate will be translated into the smoothness of reformation. If you lose the mandate, that means your view and the ideas you bring to the government are rejected.* Bila isyarat jelas tidak dipersetujui, maka kalau saya jadi pegawai kerajaan atau menteri lain, *I shouldn't pay that much attention because this is not what the Prime Minister wants or he does not have a good endorsement from the Prime Minister.* So if I stay, it is not going to be smooth sailing and it will drag everyone down.

Having said that, what is the function of the Ministry of Economy? Kementerian Ekonomi is the macro planner—ia merancang untuk *mid-term* dan *long-term*. Jadi segala perkara yang saya rancang perlu dijadikan keputusan dasar terlebih dahulu melalui Jemaah Menteri. Bila dah jadi keputusan dasar, *it will go to other ministries.*

From the beginning, in December 2022, I informed the ministry that if you want to do reform, you must hit the ground running. Most basic decisions need to be made as soon as possible in the first two years of the term, because tenure has its own clock. Let's say one term is four and a half or five years. Why couldn't major decisions be implemented before this? Because ministers spent two years on honeymoon wanting to study first, thinking they'd make decisions in year three. When a decision is reform-oriented, it challenges the status quo and stakeholders disagree. Doing it in year three makes ministers afraid of voter backlash, so it gets postponed.

For example, look at multi-tier levy management to reduce foreign workers—that decision was postponed for years. That's why I told the ministry from the start that we must conclude our policy initiatives within two years, supaya keputusan dibuat dan boleh dilaksanakan pada tahun kedua dan ketiga tanpa risau pasal undi.

If you look at the major initiatives, I would say almost 80% daripada perancangan polisi inisiatif yang saya tetapkan untuk Kementerian Ekonomi telah mendapat keputusan dasar dan diluluskan. Soal peralihan tenaga yang cukup penting; dasar *labor market reform* (gaji progresif); pengukuhan kedudukan fiskal seperti subsidi bersasar; peralihan kepada kecakapan kerajaan (Akta Iltizam); penubuhan Kementerian Digital dan *GovTech*; enjin pertumbuhan baru seperti ekosistem cip, JS-SEZ, IPR untuk membantu pendapatan rakyat, serta IKR untuk memudahkan peruntukan infrastruktur rakyat. Semua dah buat sebenarnya.

Arahan saya kepada Kementerian Ekonomi dalam dua tahun terakhir ini, yang tinggal hanyalah RMK-13 dan Akta Anti-Ali Baba (Rent-Seeking)—yang itu mengambil masa sikit kerana pelbagai pandangan. RMK-13 pun telah disiapkan, dibentangkan di kabinet, dan akan dibentangkan di Parlimen pada bulan Julai ini.

Dari segi senarai dasar, kerana kami di Kementerian Ekonomi adalah pemikir dan perancang makro, semua itu telah disiapkan dan diserahkan kepada kementerian pelaksana. Peralihan tenaga dilaksanakan oleh PETRA, dasar gaji progresif oleh KESUMA.
