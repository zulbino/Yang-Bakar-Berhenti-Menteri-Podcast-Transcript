  Haziq and co-host Farhan (Pa'an). The episode opens with a lighthearted "dilema"
  about Haziq possibly having to resign as moderator due to complaints that a civil
  servant is hosting a government-critical podcast. The Ber-ia segment mocks political
  statements (UMNO Kedah delegate on rice supply, KJ-related comments, and the Fleximart
  "T" logo controversy). The Standing Order segment covers Rafizi's strong objection
  to new Parliament rules requiring approval for press conferences, which he calls
  regressive and plans to formally protest to Speaker Tan Sri Johari Abdul. The economy
  segment dissects Malaysia's GDP growth (4.9% for 2025), Ringgit strengthening, Bursa
  performance, and warns against complacency, comparing Malaysia unfavorably to Vietnam,
  Singapore, and other regional peers, and previewing a parliamentary speech on the
  fiscal deficit. The centerpiece is an extensive, data-heavy discussion of the newly
  launched Rancangan Pendidikan Malaysia (RPM) 2026-2035, benchmarked against the
  2010 Education Blueprint. Rafizi presents PISA, TIMSS, and Primary Learning Metrics
  data showing Malaysia underperforms despite adequate teacher ratios, class sizes,
  and spending compared internationally, attributing gaps to curriculum (low maths
  teaching hours) and instructional efficiency rather than resources. He explains
  the RMK-13 rationale behind starting school at age 6, universal free preschool,
  addressing Malaysia's aging population crisis, and the bureaucratic resistance/turf
  wars within the Ministry of Education that blocked preschool prioritization and
  teacher licensing reforms. The show closes with lighter political commentary on
  UMNO's "Rumah Bangsa," coalition politics, and a jab at Shamsul Iskandar's podcast
  appearance calling for action against Rafizi, followed by a comedic resolution of
  the Haziq resignation storyline.
