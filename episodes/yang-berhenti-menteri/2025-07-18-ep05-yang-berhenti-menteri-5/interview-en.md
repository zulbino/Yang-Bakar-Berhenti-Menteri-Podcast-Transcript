Furthermore, when we give directly to district councils, this will result in less political control — under the old system, going through the State Development Office, going through ICU, everything could be controlled. That continued as usual — meaning every year ICU would submit funding applications to the Ministry of Economy for projects managed by ICU in their respective states, under the Prime Minister's Department, they prefer it that way.

But actually, under the Ministry of Economy, since the post-Independence era, every year district councils throughout Malaysia have requested assistance from the government for small projects, because I think only 3-4 district councils in Malaysia have their own money to build infrastructure — their door tax collections and so on are barely enough to pay salaries. That's why state governments too, since almost all state governments don't have sufficient revenue for development spending that can be given to municipal councils, these small projects get neglected.

That's why, in rural areas, sometimes even in urban areas themselves, the public doesn't understand — building a small bridge costing RM200,000 can take ten years without approval. Sometimes I've seen a minister come see me, "can you please look into a RM200,000 approval in my area?" That shouldn't happen, because at the same time, an allocation of around RM100 million is indeed paid by the federal government through the Ministry of Economy to all district councils. The difference is, he writes a letter to the minister instead.

So when that letter comes in, I'd be in the office in the first month of the year, and letters would be "flying in" like this every day, everyone asking for approval to build a road like this. I used to be an elected representative myself, and honestly, if we let 100% of applications be decided by the district council or government officers, it's not necessarily exactly what the people want, because it follows what the officer wants. So we introduced IKR — meaning there's a new channel. The usual allocation from municipal council applications gets split into two: one, the municipal council applies directly for projects under RM500,000, we process it as usual, there's an application process; but for the public, they can also apply directly.

When the public applies, some also ask "I applied a long time ago, it's been approved but why hasn't it been built yet" — when they ask, we'll investigate, because there are contractors who ask for projects that aren't actually needed. So officers will verify that the project is legitimate, that the location is correct. Once we see everything checks out, we offer it to the municipal or town council to implement. If they do it, we give approval. There are also places where we've approved but the district council doesn't want to build it — I made a rule, if the district council is lazy, if we've already approved three or four projects for the public but they don't want to build them, fine, even though there's approval, I won't approve their subsequent applications.

That's why the public applies directly.
