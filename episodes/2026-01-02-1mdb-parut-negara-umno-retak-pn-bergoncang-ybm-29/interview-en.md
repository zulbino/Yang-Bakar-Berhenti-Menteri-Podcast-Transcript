# Interview (English)

**Haziq:** Assalamualaikum and welcome to episode 29 of *Podcast Yang Bakal Menteri*. As usual, joining us is Saudara Rafizi Ramli—who this time acts as a political analyst—and another guest of ours, Saudara Iqbal.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Is it only this time that I get to be a political analyst?

**Haziq:** Because our topic this time is packed with political issues from start to finish.

**Iqbal:** Coincidentally, the three of us are wearing Perikatan Nasional (PN) colored shirts today.

**Rafizi Ramli:** Yes, purely coincidental. I was just trying to fit back into this 2023 baju Melayu, but it seems it's still quite tight.

**Haziq:** Before we start, YB, where did you celebrate New Year's Eve?

**Rafizi Ramli:** I just stayed at home watching *New Scotland Yard Files* on YouTube, about the "Murder in the Village" case in the UK. The three of us at home were watching different shows on our respective TVs. My wife and child went out onto the balcony for a moment to watch the fireworks, but I continued watching.

As I get older, I feel it's not worth braving the traffic jam to KLCC just to watch fireworks. It's better to just watch it on YouTube.

**Haziq:** How about Iqbal?

**Iqbal:** I watched the new *Avatar* movie. Although it took quite a long time between sequels, I think it was worth watching.

**Haziq:** Alright YB, let's see what we have in episode 29 this week. Our first segment is "Beria-ia" (Overzealous). The first issue comes from Sulawesi, Indonesia—a candidate who lost in the Neighbourhood Watch (KRT) chairman election took back donations he had previously given.

**Rafizi Ramli:** This is a very funny story. Because it happened in Indonesia, I thought there were no more strange stories in Malaysia!

**Haziq:** After losing the election on Saturday, on Sunday he and his friends went to the surau to take back the pulpit he had donated. He didn't bring it home; instead, he hid the pulpit under a tree so that other people couldn't use it.

**Iqbal:** I wonder, what is the ruling on taking back items that have been donated?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Malays call this act "buruk siku" (taking back a gift that was given). Luckily this happened in Indonesia and not in Pandan. 

It's just that in Malaysia, the Rukun Tetangga group and the surau committee members are usually made up of different people. But this candidate in Indonesia seems to be active in both at the same time.

**Haziq:** The next news is regarding the statements from Apandi Ali and Azam Baki concerning the 1MDB case decision.

**Rafizi Ramli:** This one can definitely be categorized as "beria-ia" (overzealous). It was Azam Baki who was overzealous back in 2015, confirming that the funds were supposedly a donation from the Arabs after a meeting in a West Asian country. Meaning, he was duped.

But at the same time, he was also the earliest to congratulate the investigation and prosecution teams for their hard work since 2015. Come to think of it, the investigating officers involved in this 1MDB case must actually feel uncomfortable with Azam Baki. This is because his statement back in 2015 had indirectly closed the case by claiming it was merely a donation and there was no case.

**Haziq:** What about Apandi Ali's statement?

**Rafizi Ramli:** Apandi Ali's statement is quite difficult to comment on because his language is very cryptic. When he mentions who was "negligent", we don't clearly know who he is referring to, and it only breeds pure speculation.
