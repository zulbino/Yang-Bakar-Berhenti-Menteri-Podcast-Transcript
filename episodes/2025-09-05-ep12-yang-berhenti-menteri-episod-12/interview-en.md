Just because we need a law to ensure urban redevelopment is orderly and facilitated doesn't mean valid, legitimate concerns and issues should be sidelined.

The thing that I feel is most important for us to balance is the rights of non-owner residents. For instance, in urban areas, what we should redevelop are genuinely low-end apartments and flats. Nobody questions this. There is a need and we ideally should redevelop them. Here is the crucial question: who actually lives in these urban flats? Most are not owners; most are tenants. The owners bought them long ago or had connections to get them. They might live in a nice house elsewhere, but rent these out.

I recall that in almost any area in Kuala Lumpur, Selangor, or Penang, at least 40% to 50% of the residents are tenants. Sure, if I were an owner living comfortably, I'd want a bigger and better unit because I could sell or rent it out at a higher price. But when the owner agrees, the tenant has to move out. The tenant has zero right to say anything. So they have to leave, even though they are a low-income family—that's why they live in that flat. Where are they supposed to go next?

This issue is a dilemma happening in big cities all over the world whenever redevelopment occurs—what is called *gentrification*. As a result, struggling people are increasingly marginalized and pushed out. That, to me, is the biggest issue. It's not enough to just say that property owners will get better homes. That applies to the 50% who are owners. What about those who aren't owners? Surely not suddenly... So in the end, low-income urban families are left unprotected.

Meaning they want better residential areas, but at the same time their rights must be protected.
