# Rule 7's residue: contested speaker boundaries, corpus-wide

Generated by `python scripts/check_overlap_boundaries.py --all`. Each line is a place where
one sentence runs across two speaker labels AND the camera disagrees at the boundary, so the
words at the cut may be on the wrong side.

Nothing here is relabelled by a tool. Rule 7's instruction is to mark and escalate rather
than guess. Each candidate carries a `?t=` link that opens the video six seconds early. For
the caption text around one, run `python scripts/listen_links.py <ep> <stamp>`.

Not listed: boundaries the camera attests (real handovers -- the co-hosts do finish each
other's sentences) and boundaries an owner decision already settled. The tool reads the same
decision files as `fold_hanging_fragments.py`, so a ruling removes a candidate permanently.

## When to resolve these, and with what

**They stay listed until a tool can measure the boundary, or the owner rules on one.**
Re-run the tool whenever a new tool lands, and resolve only what it measures:

1. **Word-level camera confidence** at the two seconds around the cut, aligned to MAI's word
   times. Then move words with `split_at_words`, as ep53's and ep63's rulings did.
2. **A voice witness measured at 100% on short windows.** `voice_witness.py` writes only
   above that bar. The three-model vote reached 97.6% on 1.6 s windows and the owner ruled
   that is NOT identification. 1 in 40 wrong is not a resolution.
3. **A multimodal model measured on turns of three words or fewer.** Gemini scores 54% on
   that class today, so it cannot settle these.

Do not relabel from a probabilistic vote, and do not drop a candidate without recording
why. A candidate leaves this list when a measured tool moves it or the owner answers it,
and the reason is written next to the change.
